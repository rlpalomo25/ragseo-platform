## Page Purpose

Local pages serve two goals: local SEO (capturing location-specific search intent) and genuine usefulness (giving homeowners information specific to their actual conditions).

Most local gutter guard pages are thin: a generic paragraph with a city name injected. They fail because they are not actually local — they are national pages wearing a geography costume.

A real local page names the specific regional challenge, explains why it matters for guard performance, and gives a recommendation grounded in those conditions. Matt Risinger does regional content: "Here in Texas, we deal with..." or "Contractors in the Pacific Northwest know that..."

**The rule:** If you could replace the location name with any other city and the page would still make sense, the page is not genuinely local. Rewrite until it cannot be genericized.

## Required Structure

### H1
Lead with the specific regional condition, not the city name. "Why Pacific Northwest Homeowners Need a Different Gutter Guard Standard" is stronger than "Best Gutter Guards in Seattle."

### H2 1 — What Makes This Region Different
Name the specific environmental conditions that distinguish this region's gutter guard challenge:

- Specific debris types (fir needles in Pacific Northwest, pine needles in Southeast, cottonwood in Midwest)
- Rainfall pattern (heavy seasonal rain, freeze-thaw cycles, monsoon intensity)
- Roof types common in the region
- Tree species common in the region
- Fire risk (Western states)

Be specific. "Heavy rainfall" is not specific. "Extended wet seasons from October through March with heavy debris fall from Douglas fir and Western red cedar" is specific.

### H2 2 — How This Condition Challenges Standard Guard Designs
Explain why the regional condition creates a specific failure mode for the most common guard types. Which guard types fail here, and how?

*Pacific Northwest example: Extended wet seasons mean persistent moisture on guard surfaces. This creates ideal conditions for biological growth. A guard without copper-based biological resistance will develop algae and moss within two seasons in this climate.*

### H2 3 — What a Guard for This Region Actually Needs
Name the specific engineering characteristics that matter most for this region. This is the evaluation criteria section, but localized.

*Example: "For homeowners with large fir trees, the guard needs three things: pitch steep enough to carry short, dense needles laterally before they can mat; a flowing water film rather than droplet delivery, since fir needles accumulate in dry weather and need water-flow transport; and biological resistance, since the extended wet season in this region accelerates organic growth."*

### H2 4 — What MasterShield Delivers in This Region
Apply the regional criteria to MasterShield's specific capabilities. Name which guardians are most relevant and why.

This section should feel like the regional contractor explaining what he would install on his own house in this climate.

### H2 5 — Regional Maintenance Expectations
Give the real picture for this region. Do not use national averages. Name:

- What the most challenging debris season is
- What "virtually maintenance-free" actually means in these conditions
- Whether there are conditions where inspection is still recommended

Honesty here is more powerful than universal claims. A homeowner in a fir-dense Pacific Northwest yard who is told "you may still want an annual inspection" trusts the page more than one who is promised total maintenance elimination.

## The Genericization Test

Before publishing, apply this test to every paragraph: can you replace the location name with a different city and have the paragraph still make sense? If yes, the paragraph is not genuinely local and must be rewritten.

## Voice Rules for Local Pages

**Do:** Name specific tree species, rainfall patterns, and debris conditions. Specificity is the entire value of a local page.
**Do not:** Use generic phrases like "homeowners in [city] know that weather can be unpredictable." Every city has unpredictable weather.

**Do:** Reference the regional contractor perspective. "Contractors who work in Pacific Northwest homes know that..."
**Do not:** Write as if the page is targeted at a national audience who happens to live in the named city.

---

## Citation Rules for Local Pages

External proof: NOAA Atlas 14 precipitation data (Doc 113 Section 5.3) is relevant for rainfall intensity claims. Use for quantified weather claims, not for general "it rains a lot here" statements.

Internal expert assertions: 1–2 required. At least one should be specific to the regional condition.
