## CTA & Image Placement Doctrine

**CTAs are triggered by conviction, not word count.** A CTA is placed at peak conviction, which sits at an **H2 section boundary** — never mid-section, never mid-thought.

- **Three anchors — positions, not a quota.** (1) a **soft** CTA immediately after the above-the-fold answer block (**Key Takeaways** + **Your Questions Answered**); (2) a **hard** CTA immediately **before the FAQ** section; (3) a **hard** CTA at the bottom, resolving the specific anxiety the page raised.
  - **The post-answer soft CTA — the one directly below Your Questions Answered — never collapses.** It is the single anchor that survives on every page at any length. It catches the reader who got their answer in the first screen and is ready to act without reading the deep dive: the highest-intent moment on the page. When anchors merge, **this is the survivor** — merge the others into it, never the reverse.
  - **Anchors collapse on short pages. They are places a CTA may sit, not a count to hit.** When two anchor positions land within roughly 600 words of each other — which they do on most cluster-length pages — **they are one CTA, not two.** A short page with a pre-FAQ CTA and a bottom CTA a few hundred words apart is asking twice for one decision; that reads desperate and it violates the back-to-back guard below. Never add a second CTA to satisfy a checklist. **One well-placed CTA on a short page is full compliance.** Long-form pages naturally separate the anchors and earn all three; short pages don't, and forcing it is a defect, not diligence.
- **Middle CTAs (judgment, not math):** place one only at the end of a section that **resolves a tension** (completes a Trust → Mechanism → payoff loop). Never after a section that only sets up, educates, or ends on a caveat. A very long section may carry one at an H3 boundary.
- **Each CTA pairs with a trust signal** (Doc 141) and uses the Eugene Schwartz lens (channel the desire — "See if your roof overflows" — not the process).
- **CTA unit order:** **1) persuasive close → 2) button → 3) trust bar**, with the trust bar directly below the button.
- **Spacing guardrail:** never two CTAs back-to-back; never a ~3,000-word stretch with none.

**Images are triggered by relevance or a deliberate beat — and never stack.**

- **Hero:** exactly one, in the header, fitting the page's dominant emotion — beauty/aspiration on brand/conversion pages, the **problem** on problem-aware pages (cost, failure, hail). Must read at thumbnail size (it doubles as the social/OG and SERP thumbnail).
- **Above the fold:** **zero** images between **Key Takeaways** and **Your Questions Answered**.
- **Body images sit at paragraph boundaries inside a section** — never splitting a paragraph, and **never as the last element of a section (added August 1, 2026, Karen)** — an image belongs at an internal paragraph seam, not trailing into the next H2 boundary. A section's closing beat is argument (a sentence, a closing thought, or a CTA per the anchors above), never a photo. Images at paragraph seams and CTAs at section seams never compete.
- **Each image earns its spot:** it either illustrates the exact claim beside it (diagram, before/after) or is a deliberate rest/trust beat (beauty shot, install team on the right emotional beat). If neither, cut it. **Never stack** two images without real text between them.
- **CTA buffer:** the closing paragraph(s) before a section-end CTA stay image-free.

## NAVIGATIONAL INTENT LINKS (added August 1, 2026, Karen)

Not every link that sits on its own, outside a sentence, is a CTA. A **Navigational Intent link** is a lightweight wayfinding link -- "Learn more about [X] →," "See how AEGIS 5X compares to other guard types →" -- that routes the reader to another page or section without asking for a conversion action. It carries no button, no trust bar, no persuasive close. It's a signpost, not an offer.

**Scope -- this is a narrow, earned category, not a default (added August 1, 2026, Karen):** most internal links on a page are NOT Navigational Intent links -- they're ordinary references that belong embedded in a sentence per Link Discipline. The standalone, section-boundary treatment is reserved for genuine deep-dive routing: a pillar page's link out to its own cluster/guardian pages is the clearest example (the reader just finished a real subsection on one specific sub-topic and is being handed to the page that goes deeper on exactly that). A link to a general resource -- a cost page, a dealer locator, a FAQ anchor, a third-party video -- is not automatically a Navigational Intent link just because it sits at the end of a paragraph; embed it in a sentence per Link Discipline unless it genuinely earns the standalone treatment. **Found on a live MasterShield page (AEGIS 5X):** roughly nine standalone link paragraphs across the page, when only the five guardian "Learn more" links actually fit this category -- the rest (cost page, FAQ jump, dealer locator, third-party video) had been given the same free-floating treatment by default, turning an intentional pattern into navigation clutter.

**Where it may sit:** like a CTA, a Navigational Intent link may sit at a section boundary -- its own paragraph, between sections -- rather than embedded inside running prose. This is a deliberate exception to Doc 316/320/324's Link Discipline rule (which governs links *inside* body prose, mid-paragraph, per Doc 221 §3.3): a standalone end-of-subsection wayfinding link is a different, sanctioned pattern, not a violation of "never announce the destination."

**Relevance rule:** a Navigational Intent link must be a natural continuation of the section it follows -- it routes the reader toward something that section's content actually raised, not toward an unrelated topic bolted on because a link slot was open. **Found on a live MasterShield page (AEGIS 5X):** a "Learn more about SelfClean Mesh" link directly after the SelfClean Mesh section is earned -- the section is about SelfClean Mesh. A "See how AEGIS 5X compares to other guard types" link stacked immediately below it, with nothing connecting the two, is not -- it's a second, unrelated destination competing for the same moment, and the reader can't tell which one the page actually wants them to take.

**Spacing guardrail:** never stack two Navigational Intent links back-to-back with no body text between them -- same discipline as CTAs, images, and callouts. **This also applies across types (added August 1, 2026, Karen; the set corrected August 2, 2026 to actually include callouts, not just name-check them):** a CTA followed immediately by a Navigational Intent link, a Navigational Intent link followed immediately by a trailing image, or any of those followed immediately by a named callout (From the Field, How This Benefits You, Watch For This, Compare This, Ask This, In Their Words, or any other Doc 194 component), is the same clutter problem wearing a different combination -- any two of {CTA, Navigational Intent link, image, named callout} sitting adjacent with no body paragraph between them reads as a pileup of blocks, not a page. A heading alone does not count as separation (Doc 194). If a section earns more than one of these elements, keep the one the section actually supports, cut or relocate the other, or bridge them with a real sentence.

## ZERO-CLICK SURVIVAL PRINCIPLE

Offers must prioritize: **Zero-Click Survival Tools**

### Definition

An asset that:
- Cannot be summarized by AI
- Requires interaction
- Produces personalized output

### Examples

- Cost calculator
- Product selector
- Diagnostic tool

### Rule

At least one page per pillar must include a Zero-Click Survival Tool.

## SECTION 1 — COMPONENT MAPPING

### PILLAR PAGES (Doc 180)

**Purpose:** Build authority, capture traffic, introduce system

| Component | Use | Placement |
|-----------|-----|-----------|
| **TRUST (Doc 141)** | Authority trust, Proof-based trust | Light trust above the fold, Reinforcement mid-page |
| **MECHANISM (Doc 142)** | AEGIS 5X explanation, System-level reasoning | After problem definition, Before CTA |
| **CTA CLASS (Doc 140)** | Soft CTA, Education-driven CTA | Examples: "See how this works", "Compare your options" |
| **OFFER (Doc 143)** | Low-friction offers, Zero-Click Survival Tools | Examples: Free guide, Comparison tool, Interactive calculator, Diagnostic tool |

---

### CLUSTER PAGES (Doc 181)

**Purpose:** Capture specific queries, support pillar, move toward action

| Component | Use | Placement |
|-----------|-----|-----------|
| **TRUST** | Contextual proof, Immediate validation | Immediately after answer |
| **MECHANISM** | Focused explanation, Relevant subset only | — |
| **CTA CLASS** | Medium intent CTA | Examples: "Check your home", "Get recommendation" |
| **OFFER** | Problem-specific offers, Zero-Click Survival Tools preferred | Examples: Quote tool, Inspection, Interactive selector, Diagnosis tool |

---

### LOCAL PAGES (Doc 182)

**Purpose:** Convert, capture local intent

| Component | Use | Placement |
|-----------|-----|-----------|
| **TRUST** | Local proof, Reviews, Installation validation | Strong above the fold |
| **MECHANISM** | Minimal reinforcement, Reliability focus | — |
| **CTA CLASS** | Hard CTA | Examples: "Get quote", "Schedule install" |
| **OFFER** | Direct offers, Optional Zero-Click Survival Tool | Examples: Free estimate, Same-day quote, Local pricing calculator |

## SECTION 2 — CONVERSION FLOW LOGIC

### STANDARD FLOW

**STEP 1: TRUST TRIGGER**
- User sees: credibility, validation, proof

**STEP 2: MECHANISM CLARITY**
- User understands: why solution works, why alternatives fail

**STEP 3: CTA ACTIVATION**
- CTA must match: user intent, page type, awareness level

**STEP 4: OFFER DELIVERY**
- Offer must: reduce friction, provide value, create forward motion

### RULE

CTA cannot appear without:
- Trust preceding it
- Mechanism supporting it

## SECTION 4 — ENFORCEMENT RULES

### FAIL CONDITIONS

Reject page if:
- [ ] CTA appears without trust
- [ ] Offer is fully summarizable
- [ ] No Zero-Click Survival Tool exists where applicable
- [ ] Mechanism is missing when required
- [ ] CTA class does not match page type
- [ ] Offer does not match user intent
- [ ] CTA placed by word count, mid-section, after a caveat, two back-to-back, or with a ~3,000-word gap
- [ ] More than one hero, an above-the-fold image, stacked images, or an image splitting a paragraph

### REQUIRED OUTPUT (Strategist + QA)

Every page must define:
- Trust type used
- Mechanism placement
- CTA class
- Offer type
- Whether a Zero-Click Survival Tool is present
- CTA milestones and image placement per the doctrine above
