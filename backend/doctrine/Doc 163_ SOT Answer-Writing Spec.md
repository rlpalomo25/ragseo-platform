# Doc 163: SOT Answer-Writing Spec

**The answer-writing standard for the SOT sub-pipeline — NOT a page type**
**Version 3.0** | **Last Updated:** July 17, 2026
**Status:** Active - Enforced

**Owner:** SOT Agents, Writers

---

## SCOPE — read this first

**SOT is a writing sub-pipeline, not a page type. SOT answers never publish as their own URL.**

This doc was formerly "SOT Page Type Module," which implied SOT output is a page you build and ship. It is not. SOT produces **answers**, which the Strategist requests (Doc 153 Step 6C) and the brand writer **embeds into a host pillar or cluster page** (Doc 316/320/324). Nothing in this document authorizes creating a page. Wherever older text below still says "SOT page," read it as **"SOT answer set."**

**The graduation rule.** A question does not earn a URL by being important. It earns one only when it has real, independent search demand that its owning pillar cannot satisfy — and when it graduates, it becomes a **normal pillar or cluster page**, planned by Doc 153 and written by the brand writer to that page type's spec. It does not become an "SOT answer set," because there is no such thing. SOT still writes the answers; the page type is decided upstream.

**Why this matters:** treating SOT as a page type produced two live failures — duplicate answers competing across URLs (the same question answered in full in two places), and thin pages built around a single question that belonged inside a pillar. One question, one home.

---

## Purpose: What SOT Answer Sets Do

SOT answer sets are **precision answer systems** designed to control how questions in this category are explained across search and AI systems.

SOT answer sets exist to:

- Answer high-frequency, high-intent questions
- Standardize how those answers are explained
- Reinforce a single mechanism across variations
- Create consistency across all sites and channels

SOT output IS: → **Answer infrastructure, not a URL.**

It is the vetted answer to a question, written to a standard, to be embedded wherever that question's home is. Infrastructure is the thing the building rests on; you do not publish the foundation as a separate address.

### Primary Question Examples

- "Do gutter guards actually work?"
- "Why do gutter guards fail?"
- "Do gutter guards clog?"
- "What is the best type of gutter guard?"
- "Are gutter guards worth it?"

## Core Principles

### 1. One Primary Question Per Page

Each SOT answer set is built around a single dominant question.

### 2. Mechanism Identity Rule (CRITICAL)

SOT without mechanism ownership becomes **category education**, not category control.

**The Philosophy:**

> Without AEGIS 5X: You sound smart.
> With it (placed correctly): You own the explanation.

> **Mechanism is invisible until the moment it matters. Then it's undeniable.**

**WHERE TO REINFORCE (NOT SPAMMED):**

| Location | Frequency | How |
|---------|----------|-----|
| Core Answer | Once, clearly | In the moment of resolution |
| Dominant Questions | 1-2 max | Optional reinforcement |
| Never | Spammed | Do NOT repeat |

**Example (Correct - Specific):**

> "Systems built around AEGIS 5X are designed so debris sheds off the surface and water is captured across it. That's why they continue to perform when others don't."

**Example (WRONG - Generic):**

> "Our system keeps water moving by design."

**Rule:** Mechanism identity through resolution, not repetition. Use specific phrasing.

### 3. 15-20 Supporting Questions

- Pulled from PAA
- Must relate to performance, failure, or outcome
- Must allow mechanism explanation

### 3. Dominant 5 Rule

Within the 15-20, identify top 5 highest-impact questions. These must be the strongest answers on the page.

### 4. Dominant 5 Execution Rule

For the top 5 questions:

- Must be the clearest, most direct answers
- Must include strongest failure explanation
- Must explicitly state mechanism
- Must avoid hedging language
- These answers should be reusable across SOT answer sets

### 5. The Bridge (Mandatory)

Every answer must follow:

1. **Current belief** - Where the reader is
2. **Failure condition** - What goes wrong
3. **Mechanism** - The fix
4. **Outcome** - The result

### 6. Failure-First Rule

Every answer must introduce a failure condition before explaining the solution.

> If failure is missing, the answer is incomplete.

### 7. Mechanism Consistency (Non-Negotiable)

Every answer must reinforce:

- Debris does not sit
- Water keeps moving
- Buildup does not begin

### 8. Anti-Drift Rule

No answer may:

- Present filtration as a complete solution
- Suggest debris sitting is acceptable
- Introduce conflicting flow explanations

> If mechanism is unclear, rewrite.

### 9. Edge Case Injection Rule

30-40% of answers must include real-world conditions such as:

- Heavy rain
- Pine needles
- Pollen / spring debris
- Roof valleys
- Metal roofs
- Shade / moss
- Soffits / overhangs
- North-facing roofs

### 10. Answer Length Standard

Each answer must:

- Be 3-6 sentences
- Contain one clear idea
- Avoid multi-topic drift

### 11. Question Acceptance Rule

Include only if:

- Tied to performance, failure, or outcome
- Supports mechanism

Reject if:

- Brand-only
- Price-only
- Unrelated to system behavior

### 12. Answer Reuse Rule

Top 5 answers must:

- Be reused across pages
- Be slightly adapted, not rewritten

### 12B. Host-Page Linking Rule (ENFORCED)

Every SOT answer has exactly **one home**: the page that owns the question. A category-level question ("are gutter guards worth it") is answered in full on its **owning pillar**; every other page that raises it **links to that pillar and does not re-answer it**. Answering the same question in full in two places is a cannibalization fail (Doc 328 One-Home-Per-Question), regardless of whether the wording differs.

Before writing an answer, name its host page. If you cannot name the host, the answer has no home and the plan is incomplete — return it rather than inventing a page to hold it. **Check the Answer Ledger (`company/intel/answer-ledger.csv`) first:** if the question already has a live home, link to it and do not re-answer; if it is new, write the answer and add its row to the ledger when the page ships. The ledger is the system's memory of what has already been answered and where.

### 13. SOT Priority Rule

If SEO conflicts with SOT:

> SOT wins. Do not dilute mechanism for keyword coverage.

### 14. When a Question Graduates to Its Own Page

**This is not a test for "building a SOT answer set" — no such page exists.** It is the test for whether a question has outgrown its host and should be planned as its own pillar or cluster page (Doc 153), written by the brand writer to that page type's spec, with SOT still supplying the answers.

A question graduates only when **all** of these hold:

- It appears in 10+ PAA results (real, independent demand — not our sense of its importance)
- Answers are inconsistent online (there is a gap worth owning)
- The mechanism can dominate the explanation
- Its owning pillar genuinely cannot satisfy it without distorting that pillar

**If it fails any one of these, it stays an embedded answer on its host page.** The default is embed. Graduation is the exception, and it requires the fourth condition — "the pillar can't hold it" — not just the first three.

### 15. Answer Sets per Mechanism

Each mechanism should have: → **3-5 vetted answer sets** (embedded into their host pages — not 3-5 URLs)

### 16. Compression Rule (ENFORCED)

If a paragraph takes more than 2 sentences to reach the point → rewrite it.

Every paragraph must deliver insight by sentence 1-2.

### 17. First Sentence Rule (ENFORCED)

*See Retrieval Readiness Rules (Section 22).*

### 18. Heading Enforcement Rule (ENFORCED)

Every H2 must include at least one:
- Contrast
- Failure condition
- Time element (over time, after install, under load)

If it reads like a label → rewrite it.

### 19. Mechanism Reinforcement Rule (ENFORCED)

Each page must restate the mechanism clearly:
- Once in the core answer
- Once in a dominant question
- Once in the close

Not word-for-word, but conceptually identical.

### 20. Redundancy Filter (ENFORCED)

If two answers resolve to the same conclusion → differentiate or remove one.

No duplicate logic with different wording.

### 21. Failure Library Rule (ENFORCED)

Each SOT answer set must include at least:
- 2 environmental failures (rain, debris type, etc.)
- 1 structural failure (design type, intake, slope)

Pull from Edge Case Library before writing.

## Retrieval Readiness Rules (REQUIRED)

Every SOT output must be machine-readable for AI extraction:

### 1. First Sentence Rule (ENFORCED)

**The first sentence of every section must:**
- State a direct answer to the question
- NOT introduce a topic or set up context

> **BAD:** "Many people wonder if gutter guards work…"
> **GOOD:** "Most gutter guards fail after the first season."

### 1A. Entity-First Rule (ENFORCED — June 22, 2026)

Answer-first also means **entity-first**. The first sentence must name the question's subject. It must NOT resolve to a bare "Yes," "No," "It," "They," or "Because." Affirm if needed, then restate the subject as a declarative.

> **BAD (orphaned chunk):** "Yes, and pine needles are among the most persistent shelf-builders…"
> **GOOD (self-contained):** "Yes. Flat gutter guards do create a shelf for pine needles, and needles are among the most persistent shelf-builders…"

A bare Yes/No/pronoun opener reads as a direct answer to a human looking at the question above it, but it is an **orphaned chunk** to an AI that lifts the paragraph without the question attached. Per Doc 122 §3.1 (Noun-First) and §3.2 (Self-Contained Entity), and Doc 121 §4.2 (entity prominence), the primary entity must appear in the first sentence. Restating the subject is not the banned setup-stacking ("Many homeowners wonder…"), which delays the answer — entity-first *is* the answer, stated as a fact. (Auditor enforcement: Doc 328 §4C Entity-first opener.)

### 2. Single-Question Block Rule

Each section/answer block must answer ONE question only.
- Do NOT combine multiple questions in one block
- Each H2 must match one question being answered

### 3. Table Eligibility

If the primary query is comparative (best X vs Y):
- Include comparison table, OR
- Flag for linked comparison asset

### 4. FAQ Eligibility

If the primary query is direct-answer oriented (what is X?):
- Include FAQ block with question-answer pairs

### 5. Extractable Statement Rule

Every section must contain at least one clear, quotable statement.
- NOT buried in paragraph middle
- Standalone declarative sentence

### 6. Heading Semantics

Every H2 must:
- Match the question being answered
- Include tension (contrast/failure/time element)
- NOT be a generic label

## Headings: Reader-Facing Anchors

### What Headings Should Do

Each heading should:
- Reinforce the primary question
- Sound like a natural thought
- Pull the reader forward
- Stay tight and repeatable

### SYSTEM vs WORLD-CLASS Headings

| SYSTEM (weak) | WORLD-CLASS (strong) |
|--------------|-------------------|
| SECTION 2: CORE ANSWER | So… do gutter guards actually work? |
| SECTION 3: PAA ANSWER BLOCK | What most people still get wrong |
| SECTION 4: REINFORCEMENT CLOSE | The line between working and failing |

### Light Group Headers (Optional but Strong)

Group related PAA questions under natural headers:

> - "Do they actually work over time?"
> - "Where things break down in real conditions"
> - "What causes most systems to fail"

This creates:
- Flow
- Readability
- Authority

### Heading Constraints (DO NOT BREAK)

| BAD HEADING | GOOD HEADING |
|-----------|------------|
| "Why most gutter guards are a waste of money" | "What causes most gutter guard problems" |
| Too salesy | Reader-facing |
| Introduces new ideas | Reinforces question |

**Headings must NOT:**
- Introduce new ideas
- Contradict SOT doctrine
- Become "copywriting hooks"

### SOT Voice

- Clear
- Direct
- Controlled
- **Declarative** - states truth, not explaining

### 1. Compressed Resolution (CRITICAL)

World-class SOT reads like: **resolved thoughts**, not structured explanations.

**The Problem:** Stacking setup creates soft rhythm:
- "Most people ask this question at the wrong point…"
- "The real test shows up later…"
- "A lot of gutter guards look fine at first…"

This feels like: setup → setup → setup

**Fix:** Each paragraph hits insight by sentence 1-2.

> **CURRENT:** "Most people ask this question at the wrong point…" → takes 3 paragraphs to land
> **COMPRESSED:** "Most people ask this question too early. Gutter guards don't fail on day one. They fail after debris settles and water flow changes."
> Same idea. Half the time. Twice the authority.

### 2. Cut 20-30% of Transitions

Stop overusing:
- "That catches people off guard because…"
- "But the issue is not whether…"
- "That is where the problem starts…"

Let statements stand alone.

> **CURRENT:** "That catches people off guard because many systems look clean early."
> **FIX:** "Most systems look clean early. That's what misleads people."
> Stronger. Shorter. Cleaner.

### 3. Replace "Explaining" with "Declaring"

Don't guide the reader → **State the truth.**

| EXPLAINING (weak) | DECLARING (strong) |
|----------------|----------------|
| "You might wonder if…" | "Here is why…" |
| "This is important because…" | "This is why…" |
| "What this means is…" | "What it comes down to…" |

### 4. Sharpen Headings with Tension

Add micro-contrast:

| CURRENT | SHARPER |
|---------|--------|
| "What most people still get wrong" | "What most people get wrong about why they fail" |
| "Where things break down" | "Where gutter guards break down, even when they look clean" |

### 5. Hard Truths: Remove Fluff Words

Remove: trace back, tend to, usually, generally

> **CURRENT:** "Most negative experiences trace back to the same issue…"
> **SHARP:** "Most negative experiences come from the same issue."

### Authority Language Rule

- "Most systems…"
- "That's where they fail…"
- "That's the part most companies don't explain."
- "That's what misleads people."

### Not Allowed

- Storytelling
- Metaphors
- Filler
- Excessive transitions
- Soft setup rhythms

## Audience Handling

| Audience | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| B2C | Default, simple phrasing |
| B2B | Field credibility, assumes experience |
| Local | Intro only |

### Audience Adaptation Rule

SOT structure and mechanism must remain identical **within a single brand/domain.** Tone and register may be adjusted for the audience (B2C / B2B / Local). **Across brands and domains (MasterShield.com, KleanGutter.com, MicroMeshGutterGuards.com) the answer set is NOT duplicated — see Per-Brand Answer Perspective below.** MS, KG, and MMGG are separate websites; the same answer published on two of them is duplicate content, not reuse.

This removes hesitation and "are we allowed to change this?" friction.

### B2B Framing Rule

B2B content must:
- Assume prior field exposure
- Reference install realities where relevant
- Remove "teaching tone" entirely

Use phrases like:
- "You've seen this…"
- "This shows up in the field…"
- "Installation doesn't fix this…"

### HARD RULE

Do NOT create separate pages for minor variations of the *same* audience on the *same* domain. **But across the three brand domains, do NOT duplicate — each brand writes its own answer set from the shared plan with its own brand as the subject (see Per-Brand Answer Perspective).**

### Per-Brand Answer Perspective (ENFORCED — July 18, 2026)

The three brands share the same underlying question set — homeowners ask the same things everywhere — but they do **not** share answer text. Each brand writes its own answer from the shared plan, with its own brand as the subject.

**MasterShield (B2C).** The reference answer set. The homeowner asks the question; the page answers it directly with MasterShield / AEGIS 5X as the mechanism.

**Klean Gutter (B2C).** MasterShield and Klean Gutter get virtually identical questions. Do **not** copy MasterShield's answer onto the KG page — that is cross-domain duplicate content. Rewrite the question so **Klean Gutter is the goal/subject**, and write the answer independently. AEGIS 5X may still appear, but the brand named and defended is Klean Gutter. The difference between the two pages is the brand the answer is built around, not a word-swap.

**MMGG (B2B).** Dealers don't type these questions into Google — homeowners do. So MMGG does not *answer the dealer's question*; it takes the questions **homeowners** ask and prepares the dealer to answer them. Written from the selling perspective: "here's how you'd answer this if you were selling our guards" / "here's how our mechanism solves that problem." The frame is *we help you handle these questions through our mechanisms*, not consumer Q&A. Same mechanism truth, trade-enablement register.

**MMGG is also the one site where both consumer brands can appear together.** Because it is B2B and we own it, an MMGG answer may name AEGIS 5X, MasterShield, **and** Klean Gutter in the same breath — a dealer there may sell either line. This is unlike the consumer sites, where each answer stays in its own brand lane. The text is still original (never a copy of the MS answer); it simply isn't restricted to a single brand.

### Dealer Syndication Rule (distribution to dealers' own sites)

This is separate from our three owned domains. When we hand FAQ content to a **dealer for their own website**, we *want* it to match our standard answer: **duplicate the full SOT answer set and adjust only the intro** (localization / dealer name). This is intentional distribution, not cross-domain competition — the dealer's site is a channel, not one of our brands. The no-duplication rule above governs MasterShield.com, KleanGutter.com and MMGG.com only.

### The Formula Never Changes

In every case above the **writing formula is identical** — the LLM-preferred SOT FAQ structure: first-sentence-conclusion, entity-first, failure-first, the four-beat bridge. What changes is only the *answer text* and *framing*:
- **Across our owned domains** → the answer is rewritten so each brand is its own subject (no duplicate text).
- **Dealer syndication** → the answer is copied to match our standard, with only a localized intro.

"Relevant to the product" and "no plagiarism" govern the answer **content**. The **format** does not change for anyone.

## Example: B2C SOT Answer Set (Adaptable to Local)

### "Do Gutter Guards Actually Work?"

**Version A: SOT (B2C Default)**

---

Most people ask this question too early. Gutter guards don't fail on day one. They fail after debris settles and water flow changes.

That's where most systems fail.

A lot of gutter guards look fine at first. Then the first season passes. Debris settles. Water loses flow. The performance people expected starts to fade.

Gutter guards don't fail because debris touches them. They fail because they give debris a place to stay and water a reason to slow down.

So, do gutter guards actually work?

Most don't, and it shows up after the first season.

That surprises people because many systems look clean early. But the issue is not whether debris reaches the guard. The issue is whether the design lets debris stay long enough to start buildup.

They fail because they give debris a place to sit on the guard or in the gutter. Once debris stays put, it breaks down. Flow changes. Maintenance comes back.

Water creates the second failure point. Covering the gutter limits entry. In heavier rain, many systems lose control because they rely on edge intake and cannot keep up with real volume.

The systems that work solve both problems at once. Debris does not sit. Water keeps moving. Buildup does not begin.

Systems built around AEGIS 5X are designed to do exactly that. Debris sheds off the surface instead of staying in place, and water is captured across the surface instead of relying on edge intake.

That's the part most companies don't explain.

That's why performance holds under real conditions, not ideal ones.

---

### What most people get wrong about why they fail

**Do they actually work over time?**

Short answer, yes. The difference shows up after the first season, not on install day. Most systems fail when debris settles and starts changing how water moves. A system works when debris doesn't stay in place and flow never breaks.

**Is there a gutter guard that really works?**

Not always. Many look effective early and disappoint later because buildup still starts. That's where they fail. The systems that work long term shed debris and maintain flow under real conditions.

**What type of gutter guards work best?**

The best systems control debris and water at the same time. Most don't. They either block debris and lose flow, or allow flow and leave room for buildup. Strong systems shed debris and capture water across the surface so the problem doesn't restart.

That's the approach behind systems built with AEGIS 5X.

**Do you still have to clean gutters if you have gutter guards?**

Usually, yes. Most systems don't remove maintenance. They move it. Debris still collects and breaks down somewhere else. When debris doesn't sit, maintenance drops sharply.

**What is the downside of gutter guards?**

Weak design is the downside. Most problems come from the same issue: buildup, overflow, or recurring maintenance. That's why people think gutter guards work, until they don't.

---

### What causes most gutter guard problems

**What is negative about gutter guards?**

Most negative experiences come from the same failure pattern. Debris still has a place to collect or water entry drops when volume rises. Once buildup starts, performance drops with it.

**What is the downside to gutter guards?**

It depends on design. Flat surfaces collect debris faster. Edge-only intake struggles in heavy rain. Those problems are built in before the first leaf lands. That's why design matters more than the label.

**Do you ever have to clean gutters with gutter guards?**

Yes, especially with fine debris. Spring is harder than fall for many systems. The debris is smaller, stickier, and builds faster. If it stays in place, maintenance comes back quickly.

**How long do leaf guards usually last?**

Many stay attached for years. Far fewer keep performing. The real question is how long they prevent buildup and maintain flow.

**Should gutter guards be removed in winter?**

Usually no. But winter exposes weak flow fast. If water slows or sits at the edge, freezing gets worse. That's where movement matters most.

---

### Where gutter guards break down, even when they look clean

**Do roofers recommend gutter guards?**

Some do, some don't. It usually comes down to what they've seen fail. Systems that trap debris or interrupt water entry lose trust fast. Systems that maintain natural flow earn it.

**Do roofers install gutter guards?**

Yes. But installation doesn't fix weak design. And strong systems still fail if water can't enter correctly. Flow has to work first.

**Do gutter guards work in heavy rain?**

This is where most systems break. Heavy rain exposes designs that depend on edge intake. Once volume rises, they can't recover. Systems that capture water across the surface hold flow better under real conditions.

**Why do some gutter guards overflow even when they are clean?**

Because clean doesn't mean functional. Overflow is usually a flow problem, not a clog. Valleys, fast discharge, and heavy rain expose it quickly.

**What is the best gutter guard for pine needles?**

Pine needles expose weak systems fast. They layer, they stick, and they build structure. If the surface lets them stay, buildup starts. The best systems don't let them sit long enough to take hold.

---

### The line between working and failing

Gutter guards work when buildup never starts. That means debris doesn't sit and water doesn't lose flow as conditions change.

That's the line.

If debris stays, failure starts there.
If water slows, failure starts there.

If both are controlled, the system keeps working under real conditions.
