## Introduction: Why Local Pages Matter

Local pages are the final destination in your content funnel—the place where homeowners who are ready to make a decision find the information they need to act. Unlike pillar or cluster pages that educate and build trust over time, local pages are built for conversion. They're direct, specific, and focused on helping a homeowner in a particular city solve a very real problem.

This module walks you through everything you need to create a local page that feels personal to the homeowner, performs well in search engines, and drives real results for your gutter protection brands.

---

## Section 1: Core Doctrine Rules

Your local pages must follow a few foundational principles to be effective. These rules ensure every page serves its purpose: turning high-intent visitors into customers.

### 1.1 The Right Belief Layer for the Right Page

According to the Brand Expression Doctrine (Doc 106), local pages use all three Belief Layers—but with a twist. **Product Beliefs (Layer 3) are dominant here.** Problem and Mechanism Beliefs appear briefly to set the stage, but the real focus is on presenting your product as the definitive solution.

This means local pages don't use archetype rotation. You maintain a direct, conversion-focused voice throughout—no hedging, no abstract explanations. Just clear, confident messaging that helps the reader make a decision.

### 1.2 The Tension Gradient: Starting at the Right Point

Local pages serve homeowners in the **Bottom of Funnel (BOFU)**—they're ready to buy. That's why you skip the broad orientation that works for earlier-stage pages. Instead, you open directly with a **Stage 3 consequence**: a climate-specific or region-specific problem that hits home.

Your page follows this compressed arc: **Consequence → Control → Confidence**. No lengthy buildup. Get to the point fast.

### 1.3 Getting Brand Introduction Right

Because your reader is in decision mode, you can introduce the brand earlier than you would on other page types. After you've framed the local risk, it's perfectly appropriate to present your solution. The homeowner doesn't need weeks of brand-neutral education—they need to know you can solve their problem.

### 1.4 Hyper-Local Authenticity Is Non-Negotiable

Here's what separates a great local page from a generic one: specificity. Every page must be grounded in verifiable, region-specific data. When a homeowner in Charlotte reads your page, they should feel like it was written specifically for them—not adapted from a template.

Generic content is prohibited. If your page could apply to any city, it's not good enough.

### 1.5 Understanding Decision Axis Alignment

Local pages align to a **Decision Axis** (even though they use the `primary_local_engineering_pillar` as the execution field). This pillar is simply the local expression of the broader Decision Axis.

**Important:** Don't fall into the trap of explaining mechanisms in depth. Your reader doesn't need a technical education—they need to know the solution fits their specific problem. Reference the mechanism briefly to frame the solution, but keep the focus on results.

## Section 2: The 3-Layer Local Psychology

Every effective local page follows a compressed psychological sequence. Think of it as guiding the homeowner through three key moments:

### Layer 1: Local Consequence (Tension Stage: Consequence)

**The Argument:** *"In [City], the combination of [local weather pattern] and [local debris type] creates a specific risk of [failure mode]."*

**Where It Lives:** Your H1 and above-the-fold copy.

This is your hook. You must state the city name and the specific local problem immediately. The homeowner needs to think, "Yes, that's exactly what happens at my house."

### Layer 2: Regional Evaluation (Tension Stage: Control)

**The Argument:** *"The best gutter guard for [City] must solve for [local problem], which requires [specific engineering feature]."*

**Where It Lives:** The core of your article.

Here, you connect the local problem to a required engineering solution. Your execution plan must clearly state the **Primary Local Engineering Pillar** for the page before writing begins. This is what tells the reader why your solution actually works for their specific situation.

### Layer 3: Local Resolution (Tension Stage: Confidence)

**The Argument:** *"MasterShield is the engineered solution for [City] homes, installed by a certified local dealer."*

**Where It Lives:** The conclusion.

This is where you bring it home. You position the brand and the local installer as the definitive answer to the local problem. The reader should feel confident that they've found the right solution.

## Section 3: Structural Requirements (In Order)

Your page needs to follow a specific structure to be effective. Here's what goes where:

### 3.1 The Local Above-the-Fold Model

Your above-the-fold section is prime real estate. Make it count.

- **H1:** Use the local keyword exactly (e.g., "Gutter Guards Charlotte, NC")
- **Hard Conversion CTA:** A transactional CTA like "Get a Free Estimate" must be visible without scrolling. Don't make the user hunt for it.
- **Local Signal:** The city name and a region-specific data point must appear in the first paragraph. This immediately tells the reader, "This page is for you."
- **Upward Link:** Include a contextual link to the closest relevant parent page (Pillar or Cluster). This keeps your site architecture strong.

### 3.2 Body Content: The Consequence → Control → Confidence Arc

#### Local Problem Section

Elaborate on the specific, climate-driven problem for that city. You must include **at least three local signals**:

1. One local climate data point (from NOAA)
2. One local debris reference (e.g., common tree species from a university extension program or state forestry agency)
3. One local installation or roof condition insight

This level of specificity builds trust. The homeowner sees that you understand their exact situation.

#### Engineering Solution Section

Explain which of the 5 engineering pillars are most critical for solving the local problem. For example: *"In the Pacific NW, Organic & Oil Resistance is critical to prevent moss growth."*

Your execution plan must pre-identify the Primary Local Engineering Pillar before writing begins. This ensures every word supports the core argument.

#### Local Trust Block

A dedicated section to build local credibility. Include:

- **Roofing Shingle Manufacturer Approval asset** (load from Brand System Module)
- **Reference to the local, certified dealer network** (resolved via dealer waterfall—see Section 4.0)
- **Link to the GutterGuardsDirect.com 3-year field test** (load from Brand System Module)

#### Objection Handling Section

Address the top 1-2 typical BOFU objections. The default objections are:

- Price concerns
- Installation disruption
- Roof warranty concerns

Select the 1-2 most relevant to the local market. Don't try to address everything—just the things that actually worry your readers.

### 3.3 Internal Linking Block (Required)

Create a dedicated section titled "Service Area" or "Nearby Locations."

Include **minimum 2-3 contextual links** to adjacent local pages within the same metro area or region. This helps users explore and keeps them in your ecosystem.

### 3.4 FAQ Section

- **5-10 questions minimum**
- FAQs must be hyper-local (e.g., "How do gutter guards handle heavy Charlotte rainfall?", "Are gutter guards effective against North Carolina pine needles?")

Local-specific questions signal to both readers and search engines that this content is genuinely for that location.

### 3.5 Bottom of Page

- **Expert Bio:** Load the correct author bio from the assigned Brand Module (211 or 216). Place it in the Authority Layer (not just as a byline). Include credentials, years of experience, and relevant expertise.
- **Trust Assets:** Load required assets from the Trust Layer System (Doc 141): minimum 1 dealer asset, 1 authority asset (shingle approval), and 1 social proof asset (testimonial).
- **Final CTA:** Repeat the transactional CTA per the Conversion Architecture Module (Doc 140).

### 3.6 Real Experience Section (Required)

This section establishes E-E-A-T through documented field experience. Include:

- **At least one named installer quote** with job date and location
- **A problem/solution narrative** describing a specific installation challenge in that local market and how it was resolved
- **A case study template** with: homeowner name (or "a homeowner in [city]"), installation date, property type, specific local problem addressed, and outcome

This section belongs in the Local Trust Block, after the Engineering Solution section.

## Section 4: Local Data & Compliance Protocol

Before writing, you need to gather the right data. This protocol ensures every local page is factually accurate and locally authentic.

| Step | Action | What to Do |
|------|--------|------------|
| **1** | Identify Local Climate Data | Query NOAA Climate Data Online for the target city. Required: Annual rainfall (inches), freeze-thaw cycles/year, and storm frequency. Cite inline with a link to the source. |
| **2** | Identify Local Debris Data | Query a university extension program, state forestry agency, or USDA Forest Service for the dominant tree species. Required: Name at least 2-3 common local tree species and the debris type they produce (leaves, pine needles, seed pods, etc.). |
| **3** | Identify Local Installation Insight | Query the Brand System Module for any region-specific roof or installation notes. Examples: high-pitch roofs common in mountain regions, tile roofs common in Southwest, metal roofs common in rural Southeast. |
| **4** | Execute Dealer Waterfall | Determine the correct dealer reference using this priority order: 1) MasterShield dealer in target city, 2) Klean Gutter dealer in same city, 3) If neither exists, present DIY option and route to DIY Funnel. |
| **5** | Load Trust Assets | Load required assets from Brand System Module. Shingle Manufacturer Approval and GutterGuardsDirect link are non-negotiable. |

## Section 5: Internal Linking Protocol

Good internal linking keeps your site architecture strong and helps users navigate your content ecosystem.

| Step | Action | What to Do |
|------|--------|------------|
| **1** | Identify Parent | Query the Keyword Governance Table for the `Parent` of the current page (can be a Pillar or a Cluster). The upward link must point to the closest relevant parent URL. |
| **2** | Place Upward Link | Insert one contextual link to the Parent in the first paragraph. Anchor text must be the primary keyword or a close semantic variant of the parent page. |
| **3** | Identify Adjacent Locals | Query the Keyword Governance Table for all other local pages that share the same `Region` or `Metro Area`. These are your lateral link candidates. |
| **4** | Place Lateral Links | Insert a minimum of two contextual links to the most relevant adjacent local pages. Place these in the "Service Area" section. Anchor text must match the primary keyword of the destination page. |
| **5** | Check for Cannibalization | Before placing any link, query the Keyword Governance Table to ensure the chosen anchor text is not already assigned to a different URL. If a conflict exists, select a different adjacent local page. |
| **6** | Check for Orphans | After placing all links, query the site audit data (Ahrefs export) to confirm the new page has at least one inbound link. If orphaned, flag for manual review. |

## Section 7: Content & Technical Minimums

Every local page must meet these baseline requirements:

| Element | Minimum Requirement |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Word Count** | 800 minimum, 1,000-1,200 target. Beat top competitor by 10-15% (from Engine 4 benchmarks). |
| **Images** | 5-7 images. Local roofline, neighborhood, or tree canopy images are preferred over landmarks. |
| **Image Schema** | All local images must have `ImageObject` schema with a descriptive, location-specific caption (e.g., "Oak tree debris clogging gutters in Charlotte, NC"). |
| **FAQ Entries** | 5-10 hyper-local questions. |
| **Schema Markup** | FAQPage, Article, and LocalBusiness schema must be applied. |
| **Local Data Points** | Minimum of three: 1) climate data (NOAA), 2) debris data (university extension or state forestry), 3) local installation insight. |

## Section 8: CTA Protocol

Your call-to-action must match the homeowner's intent and position in their decision journey.

| Position | CTA Goal | Example |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Above the Fold** | Immediate conversion of high-intent visitors | "Schedule Your Free Diagnostic" (Transactional CTA) |
| **Mid-page** | Route users who need more info back up the funnel | "See how MasterShield handles heavy rain" (Navigational CTA — links to cluster) |
| **Bottom of page** | Final conversion opportunity | "Schedule Your Free Diagnostic" (Transactional CTA repeated) |

*Note: All CTA language and offer types are governed by the Conversion Architecture Module (Doc 140). No local page may use a CTA class not defined in that module.*

## Section 9: Dynamic Conversion Paths (Next Best Action)

A great local page doesn't just convert the ready buyer—it also handles people who aren't quite there yet. Include "Next Best Action" CTAs that route users based on their position in the funnel:

| Current State | Next Best Action CTA | Destination |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------|
| User is not in service area | "Not in [city]? [brand] serves [region]" | Fallback to nearest active Local or Brand landing page |
| User wants different location | "Nearby: [adjacent city]" | Adjacent Local page |
| User needs broader context | "Learn how gutter guards work" | Parent Pillar or Cluster page |

### Fallback Logic

If the user's location is not served by a dealer, display the fallback CTA immediately in the above-the-fold area. The Router validates dealer count per Doc 174. If no dealer exists in the target DMA:

- The page must display **"Not in [city]? [brand] serves [region]"** as the primary CTA
- Link to the nearest active Local page within the same brand's service area
- If no Local pages are active in the region, link to the Brand landing page
