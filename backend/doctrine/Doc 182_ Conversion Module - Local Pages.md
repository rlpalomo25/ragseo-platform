## 1.0 THE ROLE OF THE LOCAL PAGE

Local pages are the location-specific spokes of the Hub and Spoke Linking Topology. Their job is to capture local search traffic, establish local trust, connect the offer to real local conditions, build product preference, and move the reader into the strongest local next step.

Local pages are often high-intent.
That does not mean every local page should be written as a blind hard-close sales page.

### Correct role definition

A Local page should:
- confirm local relevance quickly
- show why the solution fits local conditions
- build trust through local proof, authority, and clarity
- move the reader toward the strongest local next step
- preserve credibility while increasing conversion momentum

The primary conversion goal is usually macro-commitment.
But even here, the ask must still be earned.

---

## 2.0 THE CONVERSION PRINCIPLE

Local pages must create movement.

They must not:
- stop at generic local relevance
- become thin location pages with hard CTAs pasted on top
- force sales language before local trust is earned

### The governing rule

> **CTA pressure on Local pages must match local intent, page value, and what the page has actually earned through trust and proof.**

This means:
- local pages usually run firmer than pillar pages
- many local pages run firmer than cluster pages
- none should skip trust, local proof, or fit logic

### Page-value modifier

CTA firmness is shaped by both:
- local intent strength
- page value or commercial value

Examples of page-value signals:
- strong local dealer intent
- high-value service area
- strong commercial search pattern
- high local competition
- high lead value

These signals may justify firmer CTA language.
They do not justify premature hard-close behavior.

---

## 3.0 THE TWO REQUIRED TESTS

Every Local page must be able to pass both tests.

### 1. LLM Recommend Test
Would an LLM, using this page as a source, recommend this local provider or solution as the best answer rather than merely mention it as a nearby option?

A local page fails this test if:
- it confirms the city but does not create real preference
- it reads like a generic location variant
- it builds mention value without recommendation value

### 2. Click Compulsion Test
Would a human reader, having read this page, feel that not clicking, calling, or requesting the next step leaves value on the table?

A local page fails this test if:
- it confirms relevance but creates no action momentum
- the CTA is harder than the trust the page has earned
- the page feels templated and low-trust
- the page ends with location repetition instead of movement

## 4.0 CTA PRESSURE BY LOCAL INTENT

CTA pressure on Local pages is driven by local intent first, then adjusted by page value.

### A. Standard Local Intent Page
Typical state:
- reader wants a provider in their area
- reader is looking for fit, legitimacy, and convenience
- reader is often later-stage, but still needs trust

CTA gradient:
- top: hybrid or firm local CTA
- middle: strong CTA after local proof
- bottom: strongest earned CTA

Best asks:
- get a local estimate
- schedule a local inspection
- talk to the installer serving your area
- see whether your roofline is a fit

### B. Higher-Value or Higher-Difficulty Local Page
Typical state:
- high commercial value
- strong local competition
- page likely needs firmer action language once trust is established

CTA gradient:
- top: firm CTA if local trust is established quickly
- middle: strong CTA tied to proof
- bottom: strongest earned CTA

### Rule
Even on high-intent local pages:
- do not hard-close before local proof
- do not assume trust simply because the page is local
- do not let urgency outrun credibility

## 5.0 CTA PLACEMENT AND CADENCE

Local pages are usually shorter-form. CTA placement must be prominent, but still earned.

### Recommended baseline cadence

| Placement | CTA Type | Goal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top of Page** | Hybrid or Firm Local CTA | Establish local relevance and next step | Must feel credible, not pasted-on |
| **Middle of Page** | Strong CTA | Convert local proof into movement | Best place for proof-backed local ask |
| **Bottom of Page** | Strongest CTA on page | Capture highest-intent reader | Must feel like the logical next move |

### Top-of-page rule
The top CTA may be firm on Local pages.
But it still must sit on top of:
- clear local relevance
- trustworthy framing
- believable fit

### Middle-of-page rule
The middle CTA should appear after:
- a local proof block
- local weather or local condition fit
- local review or installation evidence
- trust-building content

### Bottom-of-page rule
The bottom CTA should be the strongest ask the page has earned.

Not the strongest ask the template prefers.

---

## 6.0 TRUST SIGNAL REQUIREMENTS

Because Local pages are transactional, trust must focus on local authority, local legitimacy, local proof, and the credibility of the provider.

Required trust mix:
1. **Local proof block** — local installations, years in area, local review, local project evidence, or service-area proof
2. **Local condition fit** — how the system handles the weather, debris, roofline, or drainage realities of the area
3. **Risk-reducing signal** — warranty, inspection logic, fit guidance, service clarity, or expectation-setting

### Authority rule
Authority should still feel specific and human.

Use a flexible combination of:
- local proof
- named expert or company context
- one attributed quote or review
- one concrete proof element

Do not let the page rely on location repetition alone as trust.

---

## 7.0 HYPER-LOCAL PROOF REQUIREMENTS

Every Local page must include real local proof.

Approved proof includes:
- number of installations in the area
- years serving the area
- local reviews
- local weather conditions
- local project photos
- local neighborhood or service-area references
- exact service-area clarity

### Rule
Do not use fake-local filler.
If real local proof is weak, the page must be strengthened with truthful local service clarity and fit logic, not inflated claims.

---

## 8.0 KEY TAKEAWAYS ALIGNMENT

Local pages may include a Key Takeaways section, but this module does not own Key Takeaways structure.

Key Takeaways is governed upstream by strategy and execution planning.

### Conversion rule for Key Takeaways
If present, Key Takeaways must support the page's conversion path by compressing:
- the local problem or fit
- the local condition or service relevance
- why the solution is stronger
- the local next step

Key Takeaways must not function as a detached summary that weakens movement.

---

## 9.0 ZERO-CLICK SURVIVAL INTEGRATION

To resist AI extraction and still create movement, every Local page must include at least one local action-value element beyond summary information.

Approved formats include:
- local inspection offer
- local rainfall or drainage fit tool
- service-area fit check
- local project gallery or proof asset
- local next-step estimator
- local scheduling or contact utility

### Rule
Do not force gimmicks.
The element must feel like a natural extension of the page's local promise.

---

## 10.0 PRICING AND ROI BOUNDARY

Local pages may briefly mention ROI.
They must not become pricing pages.

### Allowed
- brief ROI framing
- value framing tied to local conditions
- cost-of-failure mention where strategically relevant
- service-value framing

### Not allowed
- turning the page into a pricing explainer
- detailed local price breakdowns
- leading with price as the primary narrative
- detailed comparison-battle pricing

### Rule
Pricing questions must route to dedicated pricing pages where the narrative can be controlled more tightly.

---

## 11.0 THE NEXT STEP ROUTING

If the reader does not convert on the strongest CTA, the page must still preserve movement.

### Primary route
Move the reader to the strongest next logical step:
- local estimate
- local inspection
- direct dealer contact
- service-area fit path
- pricing page, when cost is the unresolved next question

### Secondary route
Offer a lower-friction next move:
- nearby service-area page
- parent pillar page
- relevant cluster page
- FAQ or local standards support

### Rule
Do not route the reader backward into vague education if the page has already built meaningful local preference and trust.

---

## 12.0 GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE AND NAP DISCIPLINE

Every Local page must include explicit connection to the dealer's Google Business Profile and exact NAP consistency.

### Required implementation
- exact GBP business name
- exact NAP match
- clear service-area phrasing
- review integration where approved
- local proof references aligned with real business identity

### Rule
Do not improvise business-name variations.
Do not allow NAP drift.
Local trust breaks fast when business identity is inconsistent.

---

## 13.0 LOCAL SCHEMA MARKUP REQUIREMENTS

Local pages must include the appropriate schema types:
- LocalBusiness
- Service
- FAQ where relevant
- Review where relevant
- ImageObject where relevant

### Rule
Schema must reflect the actual business, actual service area, and actual page intent.
Do not stuff pricing, review, or service claims into schema that the page cannot support honestly.

---

## 14.0 EXISTING-OWNER FAILURE EXAMPLES

Existing-owner failures may appear on Local pages when relevant.

They may be used as:
- local proof of what goes wrong
- warning evidence
- support for why the system matters in that market
- diagnostic help where relevant

They must not be used as:
- the assumed identity of every local visitor
- justification for over-aggressive switch language
- replacement framing by default

### Clean rule
Use failure examples as evidence.
Do not use them as the page's default reader identity unless the query clearly supports it.

---

## 15.0 PROHIBITED MOVES

Local pages must not:
- default to hard-close language before local trust is earned
- rely on city-name repetition instead of real local proof
- act like thin local SEO pages with pasted CTAs
- stop at local relevance without building product preference
- let brief ROI mention turn the page into a pricing page
- use existing-owner failures as the assumed reader identity by default
- mismatch CTA hardness to what the page has actually earned
