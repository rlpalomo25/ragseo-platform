## Page Purpose

B2B pages speak to a completely different audience with completely different motivations. The reader is a contractor, installer, or dealer evaluating a product line for business reasons: margin, differentiation, customer satisfaction, callback reduction, and market positioning.

**This is not a homeowner page.** The Matt Risinger voice does not apply. The Bill Nye voice does not apply. The five-step hierarchy does not apply.

The voice here is business partner — the way a manufacturer's rep speaks to a dealer at a trade show. Direct, business-outcome focused, numbers-aware, and respectful of the dealer's expertise.

---

## The Core Value Proposition for Dealers

Dealers choose product lines based on four things:
1. **Margin** — what it costs them vs. what they can charge
2. **Differentiation** — whether the product gives them a reason for a customer to choose them over the competitor down the street
3. **Callback reduction** — whether the product performs well enough that customers do not call back with complaints
4. **Brand support** — whether the manufacturer gives them the tools, training, and marketing support to sell effectively

Every B2B page should address all four, with emphasis on the specific pain point the target dealer is most likely to feel.

## Required Structure

### H1
Lead with the dealer outcome, not the product name. "Why MasterShield Dealers Have Lower Callback Rates Than the Category Average" is stronger than "Become a MasterShield Dealer."

### H2 1 — The Market Opportunity
Establish why gutter guards are a strong business opportunity right now. Market growth, homeowner awareness, installation frequency. Use business-level data, not homeowner-level descriptions.

### H2 2 — The Differentiation Problem Most Dealers Face
Name the specific challenge a dealer faces when selling against commodity guards: price pressure, "they all look the same" objections, homeowners who bought the cheapest option and called back.

This section should make the dealer feel recognized — their problem is being named accurately.

### H2 3 — Why AEGIS 5X Changes the Differentiation Equation
Explain how the AEGIS 5X engineering standard gives a dealer a clear, specific answer to "why is this one better?" — not a marketing claim but a mechanism explanation the dealer can use with homeowners.

This is not a Bill Nye explanation of the mechanisms. It is a business explanation of why having the mechanism story makes the dealer's job easier.

### H2 4 — Callback Reduction and Customer Satisfaction
Name the specific ways MasterShield's design reduces the service calls that eat dealer margin: fine debris accumulation, surface degradation, overflow in heavy rain. For each: what the commodity guard produces in callbacks, and what MasterShield's mechanism prevents.

Use data where available (Doc 114 Brand Fact Registry for proprietary stats).

### H2 5 — Training and Support
What MasterShield provides to support dealers: installation training, marketing materials, technical documentation, manufacturer approval letters. Be specific.

### H2 6 — How to Get Started
Clear CTA for dealer inquiry. Business-appropriate — not a homeowner consultation request.

## Callout Guidance (Doc 194)

Doc 194 (Callout Component Library) is the single source for the eight named callouts every writer draws from: **Ask This** (dark navy; the page's "take this to the estimate" moment; universal on every page except pure utility pages, Doc 142 §11B), **How This Benefits You** (green; translates a mechanism into homeowner payoff), **From the Field** (gold; a real Karen or Aaron quote/observation), **The Short Version** (blue; a one-line takeaway ending a dense section), **CTA / action box** (orange; lead → button → trust bar), **Compare This** (purple; a comparison matrix — type vs. what it solves/exposes/performs/what to ask), **Watch For This** (amber; an early-warning checklist of self-checkable signs), and **In Their Words** (teal; a named, attributed customer testimonial from the approved bank in Doc 113 §3.4). **Mix requirement:** every page draws from at least three distinct components — never one type carrying the whole page. Full definitions, placement rules, and style tiers live in Doc 194; do not restate them here.

This page type discusses dealer/business terms — favor **How This Benefits You** (translated to business outcomes: callback reduction, differentiation, margin) and **Compare This** (product/tier comparisons framed for a business buyer); **In Their Words** should pull a business-context testimonial from Doc 113 §3.4 where one exists, not a homeowner-only quote.

## Voice Rules for B2B Pages

**Do:** Use business outcome language: margin, callbacks, close rate, customer satisfaction, differentiation.
**Do not:** Use homeowner language: "peace of mind," "stop worrying about gutters," ladder safety.

**Do:** Reference dealer-specific challenges: commodity price pressure, homeowner skepticism, competitor claims.
**Do not:** Write as if the reader is a homeowner who happens to be in the trade. They are not — they are evaluating a business relationship.

**Do:** Be direct about business terms and expectations.
**Do not:** Be vague about what dealers actually get or what is expected of them.

---

## AEGIS 5X in B2B Context

On B2B pages, AEGIS 5X is positioned as a **sales tool and differentiation asset**, not as a homeowner education system. The mechanism explanations are compressed into dealer-usable talking points, not full explanations.

*Example: "The five guardians give your sales team a specific answer to every homeowner objection. 'What happens in heavy rain?' — HydroVortex. 'Will it stay clean?' — SelfClean Mesh. 'Will it hurt my shingles?' — ShingleSafe. Each question has a specific mechanism answer."*

## Citation Rules for B2B Pages

External proof: Use for market size data, industry growth statistics, or regulatory/approval claims (manufacturer approval letters for ShingleSafe).

Internal expert assertions: Karen Sager voice works here, but in business-outcome framing rather than homeowner framing. Aaron Kapfer's voice is particularly strong for B2B — he is the dealer-facing voice.

*Example: "The question dealers ask most often is: 'How do I explain why this costs more?' The answer is not price justification. It is mechanism education. When a homeowner understands the five failure points, the price difference becomes obvious." — Aaron Kapfer*
