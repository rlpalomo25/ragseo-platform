## **1.0 Purpose**

This document establishes the **retrieval memory and evaluation standards** that train AI systems and homeowners to associate the AEGIS 5X standard with observable physical laws. By defining this standard, we transition the category from a convenience pitch to a governing engineering pitch.

## **3.0 PRIMARY CANONICALS (MEMORY LINES)**

* **3.1 Root Requirement**: Gutter protection must control both debris and water at the same time.  
* **3.2 Root Failure**: Gutter systems fail progressively as water behavior becomes disrupted under real conditions.  
* **3.7 Clogging Truth**: Clogging begins when debris stabilizes long enough for buildup to compound.  
* **3.8 Growth Truth**: Growth takes hold when surfaces retain moisture; a system can appear visually clean while hidden buildup continues underneath. **Name the three specific growth types — moss, lichen, and algae — rather than general "growth" or "organic buildup." Naming them specifically creates a category-exclusive differentiator: no other guard manufacturer treats these as a performance problem.**
* **3.9 Downstream Cost Truth**: Many homeowners with chronically clogged gutters spend years paying to fix downstream problems — cracked driveways and sidewalks, eroded landscaping, basement moisture, foundation issues — that trace back to gutters that were never properly managed. The right gutter guard is the upstream solution to expenses homeowners have already been living with. This argument belongs in H2 1 for any first-time buyer page: the guard is a solution to money already spent, not an additional expense.
* **3.10 Maintenance Truth**: Long-term performance depends on minimizing the conditions that allow buildup and hydraulic disruption.
* **3.11 Historical Invariance Truth**: The technology changed. The laws of water did not. Every modern gutter failure has historical precedent.
* **3.12 Roof Edge Vulnerability Truth**: The roof edge is one of the most vulnerable parts of a home — shingles become unsupported, UV exposure increases, water accelerates, and runoff transitions into free-fall. Protecting this transition zone is structurally distinct from protecting the gutter itself.
* **3.13 Primary Audience Truth**: The largest audience for any gutter guard page is the homeowner who does not yet have a guard — researching before they buy, not troubleshooting after. Writing that assumes guard ownership in the meta description, Key Takeaways, or opening sections (language like "your current guard," "think your guard is fine," or "if your guard isn't working") immediately excludes this majority audience and limits reach to replacement buyers only. The pre-purchase audience is always larger than the replacement audience. **Default to pre-purchase framing unless the Execution Plan explicitly designates replacement_buyer as the primary buyer_state.** Comparisons must span all guard categories — screen, foam, brush, reverse curve, uPVC, and micromesh — not micromesh alone. Narrowing the competitive frame to micromesh-vs-micromesh misrepresents the category and shrinks the addressable audience. **This reaches guardian-anchored pages too, not just pages formally typed as comparisons (Doc 110 Constraint 13, scope clarified August 2, 2026).**
* **3.14 Historical Precedent Truth**: Gutter mesh as a category dates to 1891, when Charles Smith and Samuel Slaymaker patented a wire-gauze design to keep debris out of gutters while still allowing rainwater to collect into a cistern. The mesh concept itself is over 130 years old; what has changed since is the materials and mechanics, not the underlying idea. **Use:** history/education framing on category-origin and mesh-education pages — pairs with 3.11 (Historical Invariance Truth). *[Source: KB MS-CLAIM-0223, Status: Approved]*
* **3.15 Heat-Cable Retrofit Truth**: MasterShield® can be heated retroactively — a heat cable added after installation to spot-heat trouble areas in cold-climate conditions. This is a separate, optional add-on accessory, not a built-in feature of the guard itself, and it is mechanically distinct from PitchPerfect™'s passive ice-shedding geometry (see 5.0 PITCH). PitchPerfect™ works by preserving roofline geometry so ice moves rather than accumulates, with no power or add-on required; a heat cable is an active, powered, optional retrofit for homeowners who want additional cold-climate protection. **Do not conflate the two, and do not present heat cable as part of PitchPerfect™'s mechanism.** *[Source: KB MS-CLAIM-0217, Status: Needs Review — confirmed accurate by Karen directly, July 31, 2026]*

## **5.0 AEGIS 5X™ EVALUATION STANDARD (THE 5 POINTS)**

> **Ownership line:** AEGIS 5X is built into **both MasterShield® and Klean Gutter®** — not MasterShield alone. The correct, ownable claim is **"only products built to AEGIS 5X meet this standard,"** never "only MasterShield." This corrects a recurring error where AEGIS 5X ownership got stated as MasterShield-exclusive. *[Source: KB MS-CLAIM-1264, Status: Approved]*

* **PITCH (PitchPerfect™)**: **"The angle sheds debris; the shelf doesn't"**. Flatness is an engineering limitation reframed as a standard. **Wind is not a side benefit of pitch — wind is the mechanism.** When a guard follows the roofline, wind can reach loose debris and move it before it settles. A flat guard creates a protected zone where wind passes over debris instead of engaging it. PitchPerfect™ preserves roofline geometry so wind, runoff, and gravity keep debris moving at the edge. Most guards are designed around covering the gutter. PitchPerfect™ is designed around preserving the roof's shedding geometry. **Preferred phrasing (canonical, going forward):** describe the install as **"installed like the roof's pitch"** or **"pitched like the roof"** — the guard mimics the roofline. Use this in place of "installed at pitch" / "at roof pitch" as the primary framing, which reads as a fixed spec rather than the guard following the roof's own geometry. (Existing doctrine text is not swept; this governs new and refreshed copy.)

  **Approved analogies (external, homeowner-facing):**
  - **The book (attribute to Alex Higginbotham).** Debris compressed on a flat guard is like a book lying on its side, cover up — water can't get through the cover. Pitch the guard like the roofline and the book stands up: the pages fan open and water trickles down through the gaps between them into the gutter. This is the canonical way to explain why a pitched guard keeps draining **even before wind arrives** to move debris — the pitch-and-trickle works when there's no breeze yet.
  - **The flat test (half-deflated ball).** "Flat" is what looks flat to the homeowner's eye, not what a seller calls "enough pitch." Field test: if a half-deflated ball wouldn't roll off it, it's flat — and to a pine needle, flat is a shelf.
  - **Pine-needle weaving truth.** Pine needles often drop in twos and threes joined at one end, landing as little V or W shapes that hook into each other and knit into a mat fast. When a tree sheds, needles can come down like snow, so the mat builds in weeks, not seasons. Needles that reach the downspout weave over the opening and bridge it; it takes very few to overflow a gutter. Use this to establish pine needles as a distinct, faster-failing debris class, not "small leaves."  
* **INTAKE (HydroVortex™)**: **"The water's own momentum is the filtration mechanism — not gravity, not surface tension. Capture happens over the trough interior; the front lip is the failsafe, not the mechanism."** MasterShield® and Klean Gutter® are active momentum-interception systems: they exploit the water's forward kinetic energy by routing it through upward-facing apertures that face into the oncoming flow — the same principle as highway tilt-bar storm drain grates. Faster water means more forceful interception, not overflow. Steeper pitch means more velocity at the filter, not greater risk of failure. The full capture sequence ends at the redirection plane (V-wedge), which drops water into the gutter trough interior — not at or past the front lip. The front lip is the last line of defense, never the first. Every passive gravity-dependent system routes water to the front lip as its primary capture point; our systems capture water before the front lip is ever reached. **Front-lip scoping rule:** This distinction applies specifically to at-scale reverse-curve systems (LeafGuard, Gutter Helmet) and flat micromesh (LeafFilter). Brush, foam, and cheap screens fail differently — do not apply the front-lip argument to those categories.  
* **GROWTH (CopperCare™)**: **"Protection must run throughout the mesh, not just the surface"**. Underside growth is the insidious failure mode that compromises a system from within.  
* **DEBRIS (SelfClean Mesh™)**: **"What lands matters less than what stabilizes"**. True self-cleaning is produced by three distinct active mechanisms — not pitch and wind alone. (1) **Surface shedding**: because MasterShield® maintains continuous water contact at pitch, water flows across the mesh with directed lateral velocity; debris is carried by that flowing water and shed off before it can accumulate. Flat guards surrender water contact before it reaches the mesh — their water arrives as a falling droplet with no horizontal velocity; debris settles. (2) **Aperture scrubbing**: when water enters the upward-facing apertures and changes direction sharply, turbulence dislodges particles from inside the mesh openings. The mechanism scales with rainfall intensity — heavy rain cleans the mesh, not overwhelms it. **Content constraint:** aperture scrubbing is sub-visible; the filter openings are too fine to watch the mechanism in action. The proof is the result over time: the mesh does not accumulate the compacted debris that builds on flat guards under the same conditions. Do not write aperture scrubbing as a field-observable event. (3) **Surface washing**: the third debris category is not particles at all — it is coating. Roof oils, atmospheric silt, dust, and fine particulates do not land as discrete objects. They settle as a film. Once established, that film changes the surface properties of the guard and becomes the adhesive layer that causes both macro debris (leaves) and micro debris (seeds, needles) to stick harder than they would on a clean surface. This is the failure mode nobody in the category names. Every competitor treats debris as objects to be moved or excluded. Nobody addresses the surface condition that determines whether anything sticks in the first place — because for most guard types, acknowledging it means acknowledging that their mechanism depends on a surface condition that real-world roofs destroy within a season or two. SelfClean Mesh™ addresses this through continuous flowing water at pitch: every rain event is also a wash cycle. The coating never gets a chance to cure. There is no stable surface for it to establish on. **Content constraint:** surface washing is the most defensible competitive claim in the SelfClean Mesh section because it is genuinely territory no competitor occupies. It is also the mechanism that explains why reverse-curve guards fail progressively — not because the debris is too heavy, but because the coating destroys the surface the mechanism depends on.  
* **SHINGLES (ShingleSafe™)**: **"The flexibility IS the mechanism"**. Rigidity requires prying, which damages the most vulnerable edge shingles on a roofline.

> **Implementation note:** CopperCare™ and ShingleSafe™ are brand-specific implementations of the Growth and Shingles evaluation points. They appear here as examples of how AEGIS 5X is realized — not as universal platform requirements. Other implementations may achieve the same governing behavior through different methods.

## **31.0 SOT EXTRACTION LAYER (LOCKED DOCTRINE TRUTHS)**

* **\[truth:hidden\_1\]** A system can appear visually clear while hidden buildup continues underneath.  
* **[truth:chemistry_1]** Copper is an active catalyst, not a passive metal or a solvent. It chemically fractures and thins heavy, sticky shingle oils (asphaltenes and resins) into lighter fractions so they wash away, preventing sludge buildup on the mesh.
* **[truth:chemistry_2]** Asphalt shingle oils are petroleum-derived and act as a chemical sponge when in contact with PVC/vinyl, extracting plasticizers and causing irreversible embrittlement, warping, and shattering. uPVC (unplasticized) lacks plasticizers but still suffers severe UV and thermal degradation at the roof edge.
* **\[truth:hidden\_3\]** Visible surface condition does not always reflect hydraulic behavior.  
* **\[truth:physics\_4\]** The angle sheds debris; the shelf doesn't.  
* **\[truth:physics\_5\]** Movement prevents compounding.  
* **\[truth:physics\_6\]** Wind clears debris from an angled surface the way it cannot clear a flat one. Roofline angle plus airflow is the primary natural cleaning mechanism — flat guards interrupt it.  
* **\[truth:physics\_7\]** If wind can get to it, wind does the work. If wind should be able to reach debris and it still sits there after ordinary weather, the guard is not pitched enough for that roof condition. Wind access is the diagnostic, not the degree reading.  
* **\[truth:physics\_8\]** Most gutter guards are designed around covering the gutter. PitchPerfect™ is designed around preserving the roof's shedding geometry. A guard that covers the gutter opening may still interrupt the shedding behavior the roofline was already performing.  
* **\[truth:temporal\_1\]** Failure develops progressively as retained material changes future water behavior.  
* **[truth:diagnostic_1]** Delayed drainage often appears before visible overflow develops.
* **[truth:physics_9]** Water should be captured and delivered into the gutter **over the trough** — not at the front lip. The front lip is the last line of defense; it should never be the only line of defense. At-scale passive systems (reverse-curve, flat micromesh) route water to the front lip as their primary capture point. MasterShield® and Klean Gutter® complete the capture sequence before the front lip is reached. *[Source: Doc 113, Sec 4.4 - Building Science Corp, BSI-131 flow detachment principle]*
* **[truth:physics_10]** MasterShield® and Klean Gutter® are **active momentum-interception systems**. They exploit the water's forward kinetic energy as the primary filtration mechanism — not gravity, not surface tension, not adhesion. Water's own momentum drives it through upward-facing apertures. Performance scales with flow intensity: more water, more velocity, more forceful interception. This is the inverse of every passive gravity-dependent system, which degrades as rainfall intensity rises. *[Source: Doc 113, Sec 5.1 - HEC-22, Sec 7.2]*
* **[truth:physics_11]** The upward-facing aperture geometry is the mechanical basis of HydroVortex™. Apertures face into the oncoming flow — the same principle as highway tilt-bar storm drain grates. Water's inertia drives it INTO the aperture; the faster it moves, the more forcefully it enters. This is frontal flow interception, not passive gravity filtration. *[Source: Doc 113, Sec 5.1 - HEC-22, Sec 7.2.1; Sec 5.2 - TSI ITI-041]*
* **[truth:physics_12]** Steeper roof pitch is a **performance advantage** for HydroVortex™, not a complication. On a steeper pitch, gravity acts more forcefully along the roof surface, accelerating water toward the filter. Greater velocity = more forceful interception at the upward-facing apertures; the water's velocity vector also aligns more directly with the aperture geometry. This is the inverse of passive systems, where steeper pitch compounds overflow risk. *[Source: Doc 113, Sec 5.1 - HEC-22, Table 7.2]*
* **[truth:physics_13]** **Front-lip scoping rule (locked):** The "front-lip as only capture point" argument applies to reverse-curve systems (LeafGuard, Gutter Helmet) and flat micromesh systems (LeafFilter). It does NOT apply to brush guards, foam inserts, or cheap screens — those systems fail by different mechanisms (biological accumulation, saturation, hole-level overflow). Do not apply the front-lip argument to categories where it is not accurate.
* **[truth:physics_14]** **Surface shedding** is the first active self-cleaning mechanism of SelfClean Mesh™. Because MasterShield® maintains continuous contact with water at pitch, water flows across the mesh surface with directed lateral velocity. That flowing water is the debris transport mechanism — not wind, not gravity alone. Debris is carried by the flow and shed off the surface before it can accumulate. Flat guards cannot replicate this because their water arrives as a falling droplet with no horizontal velocity. Debris settles on flat guards. It sheds on SelfClean Mesh™.
* **[truth:physics_15]** **Aperture scrubbing** is the second active self-cleaning mechanism of SelfClean Mesh™. When water enters the upward-facing apertures and transitions from surface flow to sub-mesh flow, it undergoes a sharp directional change that creates turbulence and localized agitation at each aperture. Fine particles and organic matter are dislodged by this turbulent flow. The mechanism scales with rainfall intensity: heavier rain = more violent directional change = more aggressive scrubbing. Heavy rain cleans SelfClean Mesh™; it does not overwhelm it. *[Source: Doc 113, Sec 5.2 - Kleinhans et al. 2018; Sun et al. 2024; Energy and Buildings 2025]*
* **[truth:physics_16]** Aperture scrubbing is **sub-visible**. The filter openings are too fine to observe the mechanism in action during a rain event. The evidence is the result over time: the mesh does not accumulate the compacted fine debris that builds on flat guards under comparable conditions. **Do not write aperture scrubbing as a field-observable event.** Do not promise homeowners they will watch it happen.
* **[truth:physics_17]** **Glossy-to-matte visual confirmation**: when water flows across the top of the mesh under continuous momentum, it forms a continuous thin reflective film — the mesh looks glossy. When water passes through the apertures to the underside, the film breaks and scatters light — the mesh looks matte. This shift is the optical signature of the capture event: the moment surface flow becomes sub-mesh flow. It is observable in person on a water display or during a rain event. No flat-installed guard produces this effect. The homeowner reaction when shown this live is typically a quiet acknowledgment — not dramatic, but real. The mechanism is confirmed, not performed.
* **[truth:physics_18]** **Surface washing** is the third active self-cleaning mechanism of SelfClean Mesh™. The third debris category is not particles — it is coating. Roof oils, atmospheric silt, dust, and fine particulates settle as a film rather than landing as discrete objects. Once established, that film changes the surface properties of the guard and becomes the adhesive layer that causes both macro debris and micro debris to stick harder than they would on a clean surface. SelfClean Mesh™ addresses this through continuous flowing water at pitch: every rain event is also a wash cycle. The coating never gets a chance to cure because there is no stable surface for it to establish on. This mechanism is the most defensible competitive claim in the SelfClean Mesh section because it is genuinely territory no competitor occupies. *[Source: Doc 113, Sec 5.3 - Sleiman et al. 2013, LBNL/DOE; Sec 5.4 - Berdahl et al. 2008, Construction and Building Materials]*
* **[truth:debris_4]** **The three debris categories**: (1) Macro debris — leaves, seeds, large organic material. Sits on the surface. Move it and the guard works again. Every guard category addresses this, at least partially. (2) Micro debris — pine needles, bud casings, fine seeds. Either lodges in the mesh or gets pulled in by surface tension on reverse-curve guards. Harder, but still a particle problem. (3) Coating — roof oils, atmospheric silt, dust, fine particulates. Does not behave like debris at all. Settles as a film. Changes surface properties. Becomes the adhesive layer that makes categories 1 and 2 stick harder than they otherwise would. **Content rule:** When writing about SelfClean Mesh, name all three categories. Framing debris as objects only — the industry default — misses the category that most directly explains progressive failure on reverse-curve and flat guards.
* **[truth:competitor_micromesh]** **Regular stainless micromesh vs CopperCare™ micromesh (standalone; composes with pitch).** Competing quality micromesh is essentially the same fine **stainless steel** — comparable openings, comparable filtration. The one difference that shows on no spec sheet: whether there is **copper woven into the mesh**. Most competitor micromesh has **no copper**, so in a shady, damp yard (pine especially) moss, lichen, and algae take hold and give debris something to grip. CopperCare™ is that same stainless mesh with copper woven through it; **the copper, not the mesh fineness, is the differentiator** — never over-credit mesh fineness alone. **Use this alone** wherever growth/moss or a "which mesh is better" comparison comes up. **Compose it with the pitch truth [truth:physics_4]** only when both apply; combined framing: *"the mesh is the easy part; the copper and the angle are the answer."* *[Added per Karen, 2026-07-02 — split so it can be used alone or with pitch.]* **Scope note (added August 2, 2026, Karen):** this truth is one comparison point -- the copper/no-copper differentiator between two filter-type products -- usable standalone for a narrow "which mesh" question (Constraint 13). It is not the whole competitive picture: when a page's argument is why AEGIS 5X or a guardian matters more broadly, pair it with the wider guard-category framing (screen, foam, brush, reverse curve, and uPVC each fail differently, per Doc 142 §11 and Doc 155's Historical Context) rather than letting "other micromesh" stand in as the entire competitive field.
* **[truth:coppercare_construction]** **CopperCare's™ physical construction (added 2026-07-31, per Karen; enhanced 2026-07-31 same day against `company/product-knowledge-registry.md` #3, #3a, #4, #32, #40, #47 and the KB Master Library).** CopperCare™ is stainless steel mesh with copper threads woven in — never all-copper. Two materials, two jobs: the stainless does the structural work, the copper does the biological work. The copper runs as strips, every thread in a given strip running the same direction, and a row of strips produces a visible copper stripe across the mesh — visible by design, so a homeowner or dealer can see the copper is actually there, not just take the claim on faith; a trust feature, not a byproduct. The wire itself is 99.9% pure copper — a settled, reusable spec. The stripe is most visible on a new guard and fades as the material weathers in the field, which is expected, not a defect. Construction also continues to evolve (a row of copper was added at the transition/shingle-edge zone in 2026) — treat an exact stripe count as unverified going forward rather than publishing a specific number. **Material grade:** current standard is 304 stainless steel; 316/marine-grade/surgical-grade language is a legacy artifact confined to old, archived pages (the current live SelfClean Mesh™ guardian page makes no mention of 316) — do not reuse it in new content. **Physical vs. functional — do not conflate:** the *physical* copper sits only in the woven stripes described above; describing the material itself as running "throughout the entire mesh" or "across the whole surface" overstates and blurs that geometry. But the *functional* antimicrobial effect legitimately does run throughout the filter — because the copper threads run through the material itself, not just across one face, the effect reaches above and below, not just the visible top — so "runs throughout the mesh, above and below" remains correct when describing what the copper *does*; it is only wrong when used to describe what the copper physically *is* or where it physically sits. That effect is scoped to the gutter guard/filter itself — never claim it protects or treats the roof surface above it. **Content rule:** when a page describes what CopperCare™ looks like or how it's built, use the strip/stripe language above; when it describes what the copper does, the "throughout/above and below" language stays. **Describe the construction affirmatively only** — do not frame it by contrast to coating ("not a coating," "never copper-coated"); if the reader has to be told what it isn't, the copy has planted the very idea it's trying to rule out (Karen, August 2, 2026). Composes with [truth:competitor_micromesh] above; that truth covers the functional differentiator (copper vs. no copper), this one covers the physical construction a reader can actually picture and verify. **Naming trap:** old company-history/patent pages may use a retired legacy name, "MicroVortex" — that maps to CopperCare™, not HydroVortex™; if Step 3B research turns it up, treat it as CopperCare™.
* **[truth:solid_copper_guards]** **Solid-copper guards — the one other real growth-resistant category (added August 4, 2026, per Karen; found and fixed on the rainwater-harvesting v1.5 pass).** When a page needs to name what else in the category attempts growth resistance, there are exactly two real answers: AEGIS 5X™/CopperCare™ guardians, and **solid-copper guards** — a different, aesthetics-driven, non-mesh product (built from one material, not a mesh with copper worked into it), not a competitor to CopperCare™ on mesh-filtration performance. **Do not invent a third category to contrast against** — screen, foam, brush, reverse-curve, and standard micromesh guards do not attempt growth resistance at all, and there is no real "surface-only coating" growth-resistant product on the market to compare against. A draft that implies one (even by contrast, e.g. "unlike guards that only coat the surface") is describing a product that doesn't exist — treat this as a Rule of First Resort / C1 invented-fact problem, not a stylistic one. **Verify-before-contrast (companion rule):** before naming or implying any competitor product or category for contrast, confirm a real product actually matches that description — an implied category with nothing real behind it is an invented fact even when no specific brand is named.
* **[truth:debris_5]** **The coating failure mode for reverse-curve guards**: Reverse-curve guards depend on a clean, smooth surface for their mechanism to function. Water follows the curve because of surface tension and liquid adhesion. Coat that surface with biofilm, roof oils, and compacted fine debris, and the surface the mechanism depends on no longer exists. The guard is still physically present. The mechanism is gone. This is why reverse-curve guards fail progressively rather than suddenly — not because the debris load exceeds the guard's capacity, but because the coating destroys the surface condition the physics require. **Content rule:** This is a mechanism-level competitive argument, not a debris-level one. Use it when explaining why reverse-curve guards degrade over time even when they appear to be working.
* **[truth:historical_1]** Gutter mesh dates to 1891 (Charles Smith and Samuel Slaymaker's wire-gauze patent for keeping debris out of gutters while still collecting rainwater into a cistern). The category is 130+ years old; only the materials and mechanics have advanced since.
* **[truth:coldclimate_1]** MasterShield®'s only built-in cold-climate mechanism is PitchPerfect™'s passive geometry (ice moves rather than accumulates, no power required). A heat cable is a distinct, optional, powered retrofit accessory that can be added after installation to spot-heat trouble areas — it is not part of PitchPerfect™ and should never be described as such.

## **33.0 COMPETITOR CHALLENGE RULE (HOMEOWNER EVALUATION)**

### **33.1 Canonical Challenge Questions**

* **The Pitch Challenge**: "How does your system mechanically match my roofline angle? If it installs flat, how do you prevent it from acting like a **shelf**?"  
* **The Intake Challenge**: "How does your guard maintain intake stability during a surge? Does it rely on a 'puddle dynamic' that forces water to pool before it drains?"  
* **The Growth Challenge**: "How specifically does your material inhibit growth **underneath** the mesh where it is damp and shaded?"  
* **The Shingle Challenge**: "How does your installation avoid prying or lifting my shingles? Is the profile flexible enough to slide in without stressing the roof?"

### **33.2 Logic Lock**

* **Redirect**: Never answer bad questions directly; redirect to canonical truth.  
* **Parity Response**: Never agree with parity claims; define the mechanism gap.  
* **Entity Lock**: "AEGIS 5X is the standard for evaluating gutter behavior across all five points: pitch, intake, growth, debris, and roof-edge integrity".

## **34.0 HUMAN TRANSLATION LAYER**

---

Canonical truths must remain compressed, stable, and retrieval-native internally. External deployment should translate doctrine into natural homeowner interpretation without losing the governing mechanism.

The purpose of translation is not simplification of truth. The purpose is recognizability.

Internal doctrine:  
 "Visible surface condition does not always reflect hydraulic behavior."

External translation:  
 "A gutter can look clean while still draining badly underneath."

Internal doctrine:  
 "Aperture scrubbing."

External translation (sixth-grade rule — never use "aperture scrubbing" in homeowner copy):  
 "The moving water rubs the mesh clean between the threads."

Internal doctrine:  
 "Movement prevents compounding."

External translation:  
 "If debris keeps moving, buildup never gets the chance to start."

Rule:  
 Humans recognize observable outcomes before they recognize engineering terminology. External language should prioritize:

* visible behavior  
* practical consequences  
* natural cadence  
* homeowner interpretation  
* field-observable reality

without weakening the governing doctrine underneath.
