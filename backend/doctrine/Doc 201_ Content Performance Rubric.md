## 1.0 Purpose: The System's Scorecard

This document defines the Content Performance Rubric, the single source of truth for measuring the effectiveness of every content asset.

Its purpose is to provide a clear, objective, and revenue-focused scorecard that allows the system to identify underperforming assets quickly and accurately.

This rubric is the primary input for downstream enforcement and optimization systems.

It exists so the system does not confuse:
- citation with success
- education with persuasion
- traffic with qualified movement
- structural compliance with actual page effectiveness

---

## 2.0 The Core Principle: Measure What Matters

We do not measure vanity metrics.

Every KPI in this rubric must be either:
- a direct measure of revenue
- a validated leading indicator of revenue-generating activity
- or a doctrinal performance signal that predicts whether the page is strategically healthy

That means Doc 201 must measure both:
- business performance
- content-system correctness

A page is not successful because it is technically compliant.
A page is successful when it performs its intended job for the intended reader.

---

## 3.0 Two-Layer Scoring Model

Doc 201 uses a two-layer model.

### Layer 1: Strategic and Page-Quality Rubric
This measures whether the page is doctrinally correct and built to perform.

### Layer 2: Business and Distribution Performance
This measures whether the page is producing real-world results.

Both layers matter.

A page can fail because:
- it is strategically wrong before traffic ever arrives
- it is strategically sound but commercially underperforming
- it is visible but not recommendable
- it is cited but not moving people

## 4.0 Layer 1: Strategic and Page-Quality Rubric

Each page is scored across nine dimensions.

| Dimension | Weight | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Strategic Fit | 20 | Does the page target the right reader and job? |
| Structural Integrity | 10 | Does the page follow the required sequence and pacing? |
| Answer and Extraction Quality | 10 | Are answers clean, citable, and retrieval-ready? |
| Recommendation Strength | 15 | Does the page earn preference, not only mention? |
| Click Movement and CTA Fit | 15 | Does the page create movement with the right ask? |
| Content and Metadata Alignment | 10 | Do title, meta, hook, and body promise the same thing? |
| Entity and Mechanism Integrity | 5 | Are entities used correctly and meaningfully? |
| Technical and Agentic Readiness | 5 | Is the page technically legible and agent-ready where needed? |
| Real-World Outcome Readiness | 10 | Does the page appear built to produce the intended real-world outcome? |

**Total possible score: 100**

### 4.1 Strategic Fit

Score this dimension on:
- reader-state accuracy
- evaluation-state accuracy
- routing alignment
- page-purpose alignment
- page-type fit
- page-value alignment with the ask

Failure examples:
- broad problem-aware query treated like a replacement page
- diagnostic usefulness turned into switcher identity by default
- local page acting like a broad educational pillar
- cluster page with CTA pressure that ignores page value and reader state
- meta description or opening language assumes guard ownership when buyer state defaults to first_time_buyer — excluding the majority pre-purchase audience and limiting reach to replacement buyers only

### 4.2 Structural Integrity

Score this dimension on:
- hybrid hook compliance
- tension to mechanism to proof to trust to action progression
- structural layer order where applicable
- pacing and progression quality

Failure examples:
- mechanism introduced before tension is earned
- product introduced before evaluation standard exists
- CTA appears before trust and proof are established

> **See Section 8.0** for Decision Threshold scoring requirements — the specific point at which the page has earned the right to introduce the branded solution.

### 4.3 Answer and Extraction Quality

Score this dimension on:
- answer-first compliance
- standalone answer clarity
- context independence
- mechanism retention in answer units
- FAQ grouping quality
- retrieval-friendly sectioning

Failure examples:
- answer block is extractable but too generic
- FAQ questions are scattered with no grouped logic
- direct answer separated from the reason it matters

### 4.4 Recommendation Strength

This dimension measures whether the page is strong enough to create preference when the page mission requires it.

#### LLM Recommend Test
Would an LLM, using this page as a source, recommend the product as the best answer rather than merely mention it as one option among many?

Score this dimension on:
- stronger-answer logic
- mechanism and standard clarity
- reason weaker options fail
- product preference built through logic
- recommendation support in both answer units and narrative

Failure examples:
- page is informative but strategically neutral
- page explains the issue without shaping the decision
- brand is present, but not justified

> **See Section 8.0** for Decision Threshold scoring requirements — preference cannot be earned before proof and trust are established.

### 4.5 Click Movement and CTA Fit

#### Click Compulsion Test
Would a human reader, after seeing the title, meta description, page arc, and CTA path, feel that not clicking leaves value on the table?

Score this dimension on:
- CTA pressure fit to reader state
- CTA pressure fit to page value and intent strength
- CTA relevance to page context
- movement quality
- next-step logic
- ending momentum

Failure examples:
- page educates but does not move the reader
- CTA is too soft for the desire built
- CTA is too hard for the trust earned
- CTA does not match the actual page topic
- page ends in satisfaction without movement

### 4.6 Content and Metadata Alignment

Score this dimension on:
- title-tag intent alignment
- meta-description intent alignment
- hook alignment with body
- Key Takeaways alignment with page strategy
- page-topic and CTA-context consistency

Failure examples:
- metadata promises a stronger answer than the body delivers
- Key Takeaways compresses the wrong journey
- page is about snow and ice, but CTA is generic and unrelated

### 4.7 Entity and Mechanism Integrity

Score this dimension on:
- correct entity emphasis by page type
- same-thought mechanism plus brand reinforcement where useful
- clean first-mention definition
- natural usage instead of stuffing

Failure examples:
- brand appears repeatedly with no reason context
- too many entities activated on a narrow page
- entity map treated like a checklist instead of a relevance filter

### 4.8 Technical and Agentic Readiness

Score this dimension on:
- crawlability and snippet eligibility
- heading integrity
- schema validity where used
- alt text quality where needed
- load integrity
- retrieval-safe structure

Failure examples:
- broken heading structure on important pages
- schema mismatch with actual page content
- inaccessible core content

### 4.9 Real-World Outcome Readiness

This does not measure actual traffic yet.
It measures whether the page appears built to produce the intended outcome.

Score this dimension on:
- clarity of next step
- conversion path coherence
- offer fit
- proof placement
- trust completeness
- ability to support the intended business action

## 5.0 Layer 2: Business and Distribution Performance

This layer preserves the original business scorecard and extends it with the new doctrinal needs.

| KPI | Benchmark | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conversion Rate (Lead Gen)** | > 5% | Percentage of users who complete a primary lead-generating action. |
| **Bounce Rate** | < 60% | Percentage of users who leave without taking any action. |
| **Scroll Depth** | > 75% | Percentage of the page the average user scrolls through. |
| **Page-Level ROI** | > 0 | Revenue generated by the page minus cost to create and promote it. |
| **AEO Citation Frequency** | > 3 per article | Number of times the article is cited as a source in answer-engine environments. Necessary condition — not sufficient on its own. A page that is cited but not preferred, clicked, or converting has not met the standard of this rubric. See AI Recommendation Quality. |
| **Distribution Reach** | > 10,000 impressions/mo | Total impressions through paid and organic distribution channels. |
| **Seeding Effectiveness Rate** | > 5% | Percentage of seeded content that results in a tangible outcome. |
| **Off-Page Mentions** | > 2 per month | Number of mentions or links from outside websites and platforms. |
| **AI Recommendation Quality** | Positive trend | Whether the page appears in AI responses as a preferred answer, not only a cited source. |
| **Next-Step Movement Rate** | Positive trend | Percentage of users who advance to the intended next page, tool, or action. |
| **CTA Interaction Rate** | Positive trend | Percentage of users who engage with the page's CTA path. |
| **Qualified Traffic Fit** | Positive trend | Whether the traffic arriving matches the intended reader and page mission. |

### Clarification

Some of these metrics will be hard benchmarks.
Some will be directional signals depending on available instrumentation.

The important change is this:
Doc 201 no longer treats citation frequency alone as enough.
It must also detect whether the page is:
- being preferred
- being clicked from
- producing movement
- contributing to conversion

## 6.0 Pass Thresholds

### Layer 1 Strategic Score

| Score Range | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 90-100 | Strong performer | Preserve, monitor, scale patterns |
| 80-89 | Healthy with improvement opportunities | Optimize selectively |
| 70-79 | Mixed performer | Review weak dimensions and revise |
| 60-69 | Underperforming | Rewrite targeted sections |
| Below 60 | Failing | Escalate for major rewrite or replacement |

### Hard-fail overrides

Regardless of numeric score, a page should fail if it:
- targets the wrong reader state
- fails the LLM Recommend Test when recommendation is required
- fails the Click Compulsion Test when movement is required
- violates page-type CTA logic severely
- misaligns title/meta promise with body delivery
- creates conversion pressure that the page has not earned

## 7.0 The Detection and Escalation Process

This rubric uses a dual-trigger system to ensure both consistent quality and rapid response to strategic gaps.

### 7.1 Standard Performance Breach

1. All content assets are reviewed on the normal audit cycle.
2. Any asset that fails one or more KPI benchmarks for two consecutive review periods is flagged as being in breach of performance.
3. The flagged asset is passed to the enforcement and optimization layer for triage.

### 7.2 Priority Distribution Trigger

This is a high-priority interrupt that bypasses the normal review cycle when distribution weakness is severe.

1. The system continuously monitors distribution and movement weakness signals.
2. Any asset with a severe Distribution Gap or strong visibility-without-action pattern is immediately flagged.
3. The asset is passed downstream with a priority flag for mandatory corrective action.

### 7.3 Strategic Integrity Trigger

This is the major new trigger.

If a page:
- fails the LLM Recommend Test
- fails the Click Compulsion Test
- targets the wrong reader state
- or shows strong visibility but weak movement

then it should be escalated even before long-term KPI history accumulates.

This prevents the system from waiting too long on pages that are doctrinally broken.

## 8.0 Decision Threshold Rule

The Decision Threshold is the point at which the page has earned the right to introduce the branded solution more directly.

Doc 201 must score whether that threshold was actually reached.

Evidence the threshold was reached may include:
- tension established clearly
- mechanism explained clearly
- proof provided
- trust supported
- comparison standard understood

Failure examples:
- branded solution appears before proof and trust
- page jumps from problem to product
- resolution language appears before the reader has enough control

*Cross-referenced in Section 4.2 (Structural Integrity) and Section 4.4 (Recommendation Strength).*

## 9.0 Rewrite Diagnosis Output

When a page underperforms, the rubric should identify the failure type.

### Core failure labels
- Wrong Reader State
- Wrong Evaluation State
- Weak Hook
- Structure Out of Order
- Extraction Without Preference
- Citation Without Movement
- CTA Too Soft
- CTA Too Hard
- CTA Off-Context
- Metadata Mismatch
- Entity Stuffing
- Mechanism Weakness
- Technical Readiness Gap
- Visibility Without Action
- Distribution Weakness
- Conversion Path Weakness

This output should feed enforcement and optimization layers.

## 10.0 Scoring Worksheet

### Required questions
- Did the page target the correct reader state?
- Did the page target the correct evaluation state?
- Did the page follow the correct structural sequence?
- Do the answer blocks support both extraction and recommendation where required?
- Does the page pass the LLM Recommend Test?
- Does the page pass the Click Compulsion Test?
- Does CTA language match reader state, page value, and page context?
- Do title, meta, Key Takeaways, and body all promise the same thing?
- Are entities used selectively and meaningfully?
- Is the page technically sound enough for retrieval and agentic access?
- Is the page producing or trending toward the intended real-world job?

---

## 11.0 One-Sentence Summary

Doc 201 measures whether content is not only visible and compliant, but strategically correct, recommendation-worthy, movement-producing, distribution-capable, and commercially useful enough to perform the job it was designed to do.
