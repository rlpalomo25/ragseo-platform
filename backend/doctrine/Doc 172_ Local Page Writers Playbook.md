## Introduction: Why Local Pages Exist

Every local page has one purpose: to turn a high-intent visitor from a specific city into a qualified lead. Not to educate, not to build brand awareness—just to convert. Every word, every image, and every call-to-action exists to serve this goal.

This playbook combines the rules from the **Local Page Type Module (Doc 162)**, **Conversion Architecture Module (Doc 140)**, and **Trust Layer System (Doc 141)** into one clear workflow. Think of it as your step-by-step guide to building pages that actually close.

**Brands covered:** MasterShield, Klean Gutter

## Section 1: The Local Page Structure

Your local page needs five distinct parts, in this exact order. Don't skip, don't rearrange—the structure is proven to work.

### Part 1: Above the Fold (The First Impression)

This is where visitors decide whether to stay or leave. Make it count.

**Key Takeaways Realization-Moment Rule — Stakes-Setter Standard**

Do not write Key Takeaways as a summary of the page. Do not write it as a cliffhanger that withholds the answer.

Write it as a stakes-setter: show the homeowner the overlooked problem, why it matters to their home, and the direction of the answer.

Each bullet should move the reader from:
"I came with a question."
to:
"I understand why this question matters more than I thought."

Favor concrete homeowner images, visible consequences, and plain-language cause-and-effect over abstract mechanism labels.

**Bullet arc (MANDATORY):** Bullets should form a directed arc — not a parallel list of stakes statements. Open with the problem the homeowner did not know to look for but will immediately recognize. Close with enough proof or direction that the full page feels worth the read, not just interesting.

**Sequencing rules for the arc:**
- **Orient before disturbing:** The opening bullet establishes something the reader already knows to be true. Bullet 2 shows how that truth is being violated. The problem needs a foundation before it can land.
- **Question before proof in the closing bullet:** When you have both an evaluation question and a proof point, the question is the climax. The proof validates the question, not the other way around.

**Reject Key Takeaways if it:**
- only previews sections;
- names proprietary mechanisms without a lived problem;
- creates curiosity without direction;
- gives technical claims without a homeowner-visible consequence.

**AEO Context:** Realization-moment Key Takeaways blocks are more AI-citable than flat summaries because they encode problem, cause, and consequence in a single compact unit — the exact format AI uses when answering questions like “why does this fail?” or “what should I know before choosing a gutter guard?” For each bullet, ask: does this answer one of those questions? If not, rewrite it.

Key Takeaways should give enough answer to build trust, but enough stakes to make the full page feel necessary.

- **H1 Title:** Use your exact local keyword (example: "Gutter Guards Charlotte, NC")
- **Opening Paragraph:** Lead with the city name and the homeowner's main pain point—right in the first sentence
- **Primary CTA:** Place your main transactional call-to-action where it's visible without scrolling
- **Local Trust Signal:** Show a specific, verifiable proof point (like a local installation count or review) above the fold
- **Upward Link:** Connect back to your parent Pillar or Cluster page

### Part 2: The Problem & The Solution

Now show them you understand their specific situation—and that you have exactly what they need.

- **Local Problem (max 200 words):** Dive into what makes this city different. Use climate data, the type of debris in the area, and installation insights specific to this market
- **Engineering Solution:** Identify which of the 5 Engineering Pillars solves their problem best (this comes from your Execution Contract as `Primary_Local_Engineering_Pillar`)
- **Mechanism Introduction:** Name the specific Guardian from the AEGIS 5X™ system that delivers on that pillar

### Part 3: Local Trust Block

This is where you prove you're the real deal—in their neighborhood.

- **Local Dealer Introduction:** Feature the certified dealer by name, with their photo
- **Authority Logos:** Display logos from at least 5 major shingle manufacturers who approve the installation
- **Social Proof:** Include at least one detailed testimonial from a customer in the same city or a neighboring one

### Part 4: Objection Handling & Final Push

Remove the last barriers. Then ask for the sale.

- **BOFU Objections:** Address the 1-2 most common concerns homeowners in this market have before buying (see Trust Layer System, Doc 141)
- **Final CTA:** Repeat your primary transactional CTA with the full value proposition attached

### Part 5: Service Area & Visuals

Reinforce local relevance for both search engines and real homeowners.

- **Service Area Section:** Title it "Our [State] Service Area" and list nearby cities and counties
- **Visual:** Include a service area map or a local installation photo
- **Local FAQ:** Add 5-10 hyper-local frequently asked questions

Here's exactly how to build a new local page, step by step.

### Step 1: Load the Execution Contract

The Router provides your blueprint. This contains:

### Step 2: Gather Local Data

- Climate information for the area

### Step 3: Write the Page

Follow the 5-part structure from Section 1. Apply all content, CTA, and trust rules from the relevant modules.

### Step 4: Run Final QA

Check your work against the **Pre-Publish QA Checklist (Doc 210)**. Verify every structural, content, and conversion requirement is met.

## Section 4: Quality Assurance

Before publishing, verify these seven points:

1. **Structure:** All five parts are present and in order
2. **Decision Axis:** The opening aligns with the contract's `decision_axis`
3. **CTA Compliance:** All calls-to-action are transactional-class, placed correctly per Doc 140
4. **Trust Compliance:** Minimum 1 dealer asset, 1 authority asset, 1 social proof asset—placed per Doc 141
5. **Local Data:** All three required data points (climate, debris, installation) appear in Part 2
6. **Word Limits:** No explanatory section exceeds 200 words
7. **Visuals:** Dealer photo and service area map or installation photo are included
