# Doc 110: Constraint System
**Version:** 6.3 | **Last Updated: August 6, 2026** | **Full version history:** `RAGSEO Changelog.csv` (moved out of this file August 6, 2026, Karen — agent-facing docs carry no changelog bloat; see that file's rows for this document's name).

> **Quick Reference**
> **Owns:** The hard limits on time, length, format, and non-negotiable system constraints for every phase of production.
> **Rule 1:** Constraints are not suggestions. Violating a constraint means the output is rejected.
> **Rule 2:** Speed is a feature. Time limits must be enforced to prevent over-engineering.
> **Rule 3:** Format constraints ensure machine readability. No deviation is permitted.
> **Rule 4:** Strategic drift is a constraint failure, not a style preference.
> **If/Then:** If an agent or operator cannot meet a constraint, they must flag the task for review, not submit non-compliant work.

## 1. THE PHILOSOPHY OF CONSTRAINTS

A system without constraints scales infinitely in cost, time, inconsistency, and confusion. This document defines the boundaries that make the production pipeline executable, predictable, and profitable.

We do not optimize for perfect.
We optimize for perfect within the constraint.

Constraints do three jobs:
- they limit waste
- they preserve machine-readable output
- they prevent strategic drift

A fast system that keeps making the wrong assumptions is still failing.
A strategically correct system that ignores time, format, and process is also failing.

Both sides matter.

## 2. CONSTRAINT CATEGORIES

This document governs four categories of constraints:

1. **Operational Constraints**  
   Time, length, and throughput limits.

2. **Format Constraints**  
   Required output structures and machine-readable forms.

3. **Strategic Constraints**  
   Rules that prevent the system from drifting into the wrong page logic, wrong funnel logic, or wrong execution behavior.

4. **Audience and Persuasion Constraints**  
   Rules that protect reader-state accuracy, emotional-fit accuracy, and conversion-fit accuracy.

All four categories are mandatory.

## 3. STANDING STRATEGIC CONSTRAINTS

These constraints apply across the full pipeline unless an explicit directive overrides them.

### Constraint 1: Do not assume the wrong reader identity
Content must not assume the reader owns or has owned a gutter guard unless the keyword, routing decision, or explicit directive clearly supports that assumption.

### Constraint 2: Do not default to replacement framing
Broad problem-led pages must not be built as if the primary reader is already replacing a failed system.

### Constraint 3: Do not collapse problem awareness into evaluation intent
Problem-aware does not automatically mean:
- solution-comparing
- brand-comparing
- replacement-ready

### Constraint 4: Do not default to comparison mode
Comparison logic requires positive evidence such as:
- vs
- best
- reviews
- alternatives
- competitor names
- switch, replace, or upgrade language

### Constraint 5: Do not mistake information gain for success
A page that informs but fails to create preference and action-readiness is not compliant with system doctrine.

### Constraint 6: Do not force one emotional arc onto every page
Broad problem-aware readers, evaluation-aware readers, and diagnostic or replacement readers do not all follow the same emotional sequence.

### Constraint 7: Do not introduce the product before the page earns it
The product may not be introduced until the page has created enough explanation, proof, and trust to make that introduction feel earned.

### Constraint 8: Do not let the page stop at education when preference is required
If the page is intended to influence product choice, it must move beyond explanation into preference, trust, and action-readiness.

### Constraint 9: Do not mismatch CTA pressure to reader readiness
A CTA must not be softer than the desire the page has built, and must not be harder than the trust the page has earned.

### Constraint 10: Do not create separate upstream plans when one shared spine will work
Execution planning must prefer one reusable strategic spine unless the keyword clearly requires a separate plan.

### Constraint 11: Do not skip the progression
The page must not jump from tension straight into product pitch.
It must progress through the appropriate sequence of:
- tension
- mechanism or criteria
- proof
- trust
- resolution
- action

### Constraint 12: Do not use category-war framing as the default opening
Current-owner failures and category weaknesses may be used as diagnostic help and warning evidence, but they must not become the automatic opening identity for broad pages.

### Constraint 13: Do not narrow competitive comparisons to micromesh only
Any content that includes a comparison of guard types must span all guard categories: screen, foam, brush, reverse curve, uPVC, and micromesh. Narrowing the competitive frame to micromesh-vs-micromesh misrepresents the category and shrinks the addressable audience. This constraint applies to body copy, comparison tables, FAQ answer blocks, and meta descriptions.

**Scope, stated explicitly (added August 2, 2026, Karen -- this was being read too narrowly):** this constraint is not limited to pages formally typed as comparisons. It reaches guardian-anchored and mechanism pages too -- a CopperCare page that argues against "other micromesh" without situating that inside the wider category (screen, foam, brush, reverse curve, and uPVC also fail, each differently) still narrows the competitive frame, even though the page itself isn't a "comparison page." A single-category competitive point (e.g., the copper-vs-no-copper differentiator) may still be used on its own for a narrow question ("which mesh is better"), but the page's larger argument for why AEGIS 5X or a guardian matters must not rest on filter-vs-filter alone.

### Constraint 14: Do not misidentify AEGIS 5X™
AEGIS 5X™ is an engineering standard — not a product, not a gutter protection system, and not a brand. It defines five performance mechanisms called guardians. It lives inside certain gutter protection systems. Any content that describes AEGIS 5X™ as a product or a gutter protection system violates this constraint and must be corrected before publication.

### Constraint 15: Do not blur brand and dealer responsibility (added August 1, 2026, Karen)
MasterShield® and Klean Gutter® are the engineered systems. The people who sell, price, finance, and install them are local, independent dealers. Content stays in the neighbor's voice, but it must never let the brand and the dealer trade responsibilities — this protects the brand from claims and liabilities that belong to an independent business it doesn't control.

**Attribute to the brand:**
- The design.
- The materials.
- The engineering.
- The product warranty.
- The standards it promotes.

**Describe dealers as:**
- Local professionals who sell and install the brand.
- People who can assess, price, finance, and install.
- Independent businesses whose offerings may vary.

**What this rules out:** any sentence that has the brand itself pricing, financing, scheduling, or installing ("MasterShield will install your gutters," "MasterShield's price for your home is…") — that belongs to the dealer, not the manufacturer. It also rules out the reverse: crediting a dealer with the design, engineering, or warranty terms that only the brand stands behind.

**MMGG note:** MicroMeshGutterGuards.com's B2B content speaks to the dealer network directly — dealers are the audience, not a third party being described — so this constraint governs MasterShield and Klean Gutter homeowner-facing content, where the boundary has to be made explicit for a reader who isn't already a dealer.

## 4. PHASE-BY-PHASE OPERATIONAL CONSTRAINTS

### Phase 1: Competitive Analysis
- **Max Time:** 10 minutes per keyword cluster
- **Max Output Length:** 500 words
- **Required Format:** Bulleted list of gaps, H1-H3 structure table, one paragraph summary of the AI search frontier

### Phase 2: Keyword Selection
- **Max Time:** 15 minutes per weekly batch
- **Max Output Length:** 10 keywords per batch
- **Required Format:** CSV or Markdown table with Volume, Intent, Difficulty, and Target Brand

### Phase 3: Data Collection
- **Max Time:** 30 minutes per weekly batch
- **Max Output Length:** N/A
- **Required Format:** Raw data bundle with source exports, SERP evidence, and citation logs

### Phase 4: Execution Planning
- **Max Time:** 5 minutes per article
- **Max Output Length:** 600 to 800 words
- **Required Format:** Markdown document with H1-H3 outline, required facts list, and narrative or emotional arc summary

### Phase 5: Content Creation
- **Max Time:** 10 minutes per article for AI / 60 minutes for Human
- **Max Output Length:** 1,800 to 2,500 words for Pillar / 800 to 1,200 words for Cluster
- **Required Format:** Markdown document adhering to the governing answer-format and chunking rules

### Phase 6: Quality Control
- **Max Time:** 2 minutes per article for AI / 10 minutes for Human
- **Max Output Length:** Pass/Fail grade plus bulleted list of required fixes
- **Required Format:** Markdown checklist

### Phase 7: Amplification
- **Max Time:** 5 minutes per article
- **Max Output Length:** 3 social posts, 1 email, 1 outreach template
- **Required Format:** Plain text ready for copy and paste

## 5. EXECUTION-PLAN CONSTRAINTS

Execution Planning must not:
- produce separate plans when one shared execution spine will work
- build for separate audiences upstream unless the keyword clearly requires it
- ignore the Recommend Test
- ignore the Click Compulsion Test
- create a page structure that would leave the reader merely satisfied and unmoved
- build a structure that cannot be adapted downstream for B2B, B2C, technical depth, and brand voice where appropriate

Execution plans should be built so they can be adapted downstream for:
- B2B
- B2C
- technical depth differences
- brand voice differences

without requiring a completely new upstream plan unless clearly necessary.

## 6. WRITING CONSTRAINTS

Writers must not:
- assume the page is for a replacement buyer by default
- introduce the product before the page earns it
- end the page at education when the page should be building preference
- use a CTA that is softer than the desire the page has built
- use a CTA that is harder than the trust the page has earned
- use a reader identity that the query does not justify
- treat broad problem-aware pages as if they were already comparison pages
- flatten every page into the same emotional arc
- narrow competitive comparisons to micromesh only — all guard categories must be represented (see Constraint 13)
- describe AEGIS 5X™ as a product or gutter protection system (see Constraint 14)

A page that satisfies curiosity but creates no movement fails the constraint system.

## 7. QUALITY CONTROL CONSTRAINTS

Quality Control must fail pages that:
- assume the wrong reader identity
- use the wrong emotional arc for the query
- default broad pages into replacement or comparison framing
- mention the product without making it feel like the preferred answer
- educate the reader without creating a meaningful reason to act
- violate the required structural progression
- mismatch CTA pressure to what the page has actually earned

## 8. ENFORCEMENT

These constraints are enforced by the System Governor and by every downstream audit layer that checks structural, strategic, emotional, and conversion compliance.

Any output that violates these constraints is rejected and returned to the responsible agent or operator.
