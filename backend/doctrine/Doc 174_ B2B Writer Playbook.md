### B2B Page Structure Template

```
1. HOOK (100-150 words)
   - State the business impact immediately
   - No "what is" - go straight to the pain

2. STANDARD SETTING (200-300 words)
   - Present 5 Engineering Pillars as requirements
   - Frame as "non-negotiable for profitability"

3. PROOF BLOCKS (3-4 sections, 300-400 words each)
   - Technical specs
   - ASTM testing results
   - Installation efficiency data
   - Dealer support benefits

4. COMPARISON (optional, 200-300 words)
   - Direct comparison to "the other option"
   - Focus on callback rates, failure rates

5. CTA & NEXT STEP (100-150 words)
   - Partnership language
   - No "buy" - use "become," "join," "partner"
```

## 1.0 The B2B Core Philosophy

The fundamental difference between our B2C and B2B content is how we present the standard (the 5 Engineering Pillars).

*   **B2C (Homeowners):** We *walk* the audience into the standard. We educate them on why the standard matters before revealing that we meet it.
*   **B2B (Dealers, Installers, Contractors):** We *state* the standard directly. This audience already understands the pain points of the industry (callbacks, failed installs, angry customers). We do not need to educate them on the problem; we need to prove we have the engineered solution.

---

## 2.0 Tone and Voice Adjustments

When writing B2B content, the Writer Agent must adjust the Brand Voice Guide (Doc 134) as follows:

| Element | B2C Approach | B2B Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Emotion** | Peace of mind, protection | Profitability, reduced liability, efficiency |
| **Technical Depth** | Accessible, analogy-driven | Highly technical, specification-driven |
| **The "Enemy"** | Bad weather, clogs, damage | Callbacks, cheap materials, wasted labor |
| **Proof Points** | Customer reviews, warranties | ASTM testing, material specs, installation speed |

## 3.0 Structural Requirements (The B2B Overlay)

When executing an Execution Plan (Doc 153) for a B2B audience, the following structural rules apply:

### 3.1 The Introduction (The Hook)
Do not waste time explaining what a gutter guard is. The hook must immediately address the business impact of choosing the wrong system.
*   *Example:* "Every callback eats your margin. If you're installing systems that can't handle 40 inches of rain per hour, you are installing future liabilities."

### 3.2 Presenting the 5 Engineering Pillars
State the pillars as non-negotiable requirements for a profitable installation business.
*   *Example:* "We engineered the system around five critical failure points. If your current supplier fails on even one of these, your installation will eventually fail."

### 3.3 The "Your Questions Answered" Block (AEO Integration)
B2B FAQs should focus on installation, specifications, and dealer support.
*   *Example FAQ:* "What is the water volume capacity of the MasterShield system?"
*   *Answer-First:* "The MasterShield system is tested to handle up to 40 inches of rain per hour without overshooting."

---

## 4.0 The B2B Call to Action (CTA)

B2B CTAs must focus on partnership, not purchase.

*   **Avoid:** "Get a Free Estimate," "Buy Now," "Protect Your Home."
*   **Use:** "Become an Authorized Dealer," "Request Technical Specs," "Speak with our Dealer Support Team."

---

## 5.0 Brand-Specific B2B Angles

While the engineering remains the same, the pitch changes based on the brand:

*   **MasterShield (Doc 130):** Pitch to high-end contractors. Focus on the "Gold Standard," the original inventor, and the ability to charge a premium for the best system on the market.
*   **Klean Gutter (Doc 131):** Pitch to volume installers. Focus on the surgical-grade stainless steel, the pitch-agnostic installation, and the lack of a plastic substructure that causes callbacks.
*   **MMGG (Doc 132):** Pitch to general contractors. Focus on the balance of performance and value, and the simplicity of the micromesh design.

## ENFORCEMENT RULES (MANDATORY)

### Win Vector Enforcement
- The article must clearly express the Win Vector in plain language

### Trust Enforcement Rule
- Every major claim must meet Claim Strength Level 2 or higher
- Use B2B-specific trust: trade references, installation specs, warranty terms

### Structure Enforcement Rule
- Follow the H1–H3 structure from the Execution Plan exactly

### Revenue Alignment Rule
- CTA tone must follow Execution Plan
- B2B CTAs focus on partnership, not purchase
