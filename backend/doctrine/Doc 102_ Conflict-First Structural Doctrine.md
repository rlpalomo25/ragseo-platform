# Doc 102: Conflict-First Structural Doctrine
**Version 8.2** | **Last Updated: July 22, 2026**
**Status:** Tier 3 Doctrine (Structural Foundation)

---

> **Quick Reference**
> **Owns:** The macro H1-H3 architecture of every page.
> **Rule 1:** Every page must open with the conflict, tension, or unresolved roofline performance problem, not the solution.
> **Rule 2:** The H2 structure must follow the Tension Gradient appropriate to the reader state.
> **Rule 3:** The solution cannot be introduced until the structural threshold for explanation, proof, and trust has been met.
> **Rule 4:** Conflict-first does not mean replacement-buyer-first. Tension must match the likely reader state.
> **If/Then:** If outlining a new page, the first H2 must address the reader’s relevant tension, not our product features.

## 1.0 INTRODUCTION: THE ARCHITECTURAL SPINE

This doctrine defines the mandatory architectural spine for every piece of content. It determines the sequence of ideas that creates a compelling, psychologically sound journey for the reader.

This structure is powered by the emotional engine defined in **Doc 104 (Writing Tension Gradient)**.

### Doc 102 vs Doc 104: Complementary Ownership

| This Doc (102) | Doc 104 (Tension Gradient) |
|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Owns:** The 7 structural LAYERS | **Owns:** The emotional STAGES and arcs |
| Architectural spine (H1-H3) | Psychological journey (reader feeling) |

| Referenced by: Writer Playbooks | Referenced by: Conversion Modules |

These docs work together. Doc 102 provides the skeleton. Doc 104 provides the emotional pacing.

### The Core Structural Elements

Every page is built from these seven elements in this order:

1. **Tension Layer**
2. **Mechanism Layer**
3. **Proof Layer**
4. **Tension Reset**
5. **Authority Layer**
6. **Resolution Layer**
7. **CTA Layer**

The order is fixed.

What changes is the kind of tension used inside Layer 1 and the pace of movement through the layers.

## 2.0 THE GOLDEN RULE: THE ANSWER-FIRST / TENSION-FIRST HYBRID

This doctrine owns the definition of the hybrid hook that opens every page.

It serves both:
- machines, through a direct answer
- humans, through immediate tension

> **Line 1: The Direct Answer.** The first sentence must be a standalone, fact-based answer to the primary user query. Formatting is governed by **Doc 108**.
>
> **Lines 2 to 4: The Narrative Tension.** Immediately following the direct answer, the next 1 to 3 sentences must pivot to the narrative hook, introducing the conflict, tradeoff, risk, or tension that pulls the reader forward.

This opening is non-negotiable.

### Clarification

Conflict-first does not mean:
- assume the reader already owns a failing guard
- assume replacement intent
- assume category comparison mode

Conflict-first means:
- start from the relevant tension the reader is trying to resolve
- make the tension match the likely buyer state
- make the next question feel necessary

## 3.0 THE MANDATORY SEQUENCE: LAYER BY LAYER

### A. Tension Layer

This layer uses the hybrid hook to capture attention and establish why the page matters.

### The governing rule

The Tension Layer must be chosen based on the likely reader state.

The doctrine must never assume that the most dramatic failure-based angle is always correct.

### Approved tension families

Use one of these five tension families:

1. **Open problem or friction**
2. **Hidden failure mode**
3. **Costly mistake most people make**
4. **Counterintuitive truth**
5. **Industry-wide design flaw or performance gap**

### Reader-state routing for tension choice

#### Default reader state: pre-purchase, broad problem-led
This is the standing default unless the keyword or directive clearly proves otherwise.

Use these tension families first:
- open problem or friction
- costly mistake most people make
- counterintuitive truth

Do not default to:
- hidden failure mode written as if the reader already owns a failed system
- industry-wide flaw written as if the reader is already shopping for a replacement

#### Diagnostic current-owner reader
Use these tension families first:
- hidden failure mode
- counterintuitive truth
- industry-wide design flaw

This reader is trying to understand what is going wrong now.

#### Brand-comparing or replacement-evaluating reader
Use these tension families first:
- hidden failure mode
- industry-wide flaw
- data-backed performance gap

This reader is already evaluating alternatives more directly.

### The tension selection rule

If the query is broad and problem-led, the page must begin with shared gutter tension, not existing-owner identity.

Correct:
- debris stops moving
- heavy rain has to be captured before the front lip becomes the last chance
- growth begins where moisture and fine organic material meet
- the roof edge is vulnerable even when the gutter looks fine

Incorrect:
- your old guard failed, now replace it
- your current system is broken, so switch
- lead as if the reader is already a disgruntled owner unless the query explicitly supports that

### Locked example standard

> *A flat gutter guard on a pitched roof is a shelf. Things pile up on shelves, debris, ice, organic material.*

This remains an approved model because it describes a broad physical tension that both first-time buyers and current owners can understand.

Use it or build from it when explaining why flat guards fail.

### B. Mechanism Layer

This layer explains the engineering behind the tension.

It is where the technical frame earns its place.

> **AEO Block Minimum:** This layer, between Tension and Authority, must contain a minimum of two AEO-optimized content blocks as defined in **Doc 120 (Engineering for Selection)**.

This is mandatory.

### Mechanism rule

The mechanism layer must answer the tension introduced in Layer A.

It must not feel like a disconnected product pitch.

For broad pre-purchase readers, the mechanism layer should explain:
- why the tension happens
- what design principle resolves it
- what standard separates working from failing systems

### C. Proof Layer

This layer provides performance evidence.

It answers the question:
- does this work

Approved proof includes:
- independent testing
- observed field performance
- comparative evidence
- material behavior
- installation-method proof

### D. The Tension Reset

This is a mandatory single-paragraph emotional pivot before the Authority layer.

It releases pressure and reframes the evaluation as useful and empowering.

Approved model:

> *This does not mean every system fails. It means not all systems are engineered to the same standard, and the difference becomes visible in how they perform over time, not how they look on installation day.*

### E. Authority Layer

This layer provides credibility evidence.

It answers the question:
- why should I trust you

### Preferred authority mix

Use a flexible combination of:
- a named expert or writer with relevant E-E-A-T biography context
- one attributed quote that sharpens the page's central point
- one concrete proof element

Concrete proof elements may include:
- patent holder status
- years of testing
- manufacturing and field expertise
- documented approvals
- original engineering proof
- field observation tied to real installations

**Format requirement (added July 21, 2026):** the attributed quote required here must render as Doc 194's named **From the Field** component, not plain prose. Doc 194 is the single owner of this rule — see it for the full requirement and why it exists.

**Brand and person lock (added July 22, 2026):** the field observation/proof element and the attributed quote required here are facts, not reusable creative material. A real story belongs to the brand it happened under; a quote belongs to the person who said it. Neither may be reassigned to make a sibling brand's page or a different-author page easier to fill out. Doc 114 §4.5.1/§4.5.2 is the single owner of this rule — see it for the full requirement.

### Authority rule

Authority should feel specific and human, not generic and templated.

Do not force every page into the exact same authority pattern.
A short expert identification, one strong quote, and one proof element is usually enough.

### F. Resolution Layer

Only after the structural threshold for explanation, proof, and trust is met may the branded solution be introduced as the engineered answer.

### Resolution rule

The solution may not appear before the page has done all three:
- clarified the tension
- explained the mechanism
- justified trust

### G. CTA Layer

The final layer provides a clear, readiness-appropriate call to action.

The goal is to convert the confidence built by the preceding layers into a measurable action.

- **TOFU CTA:** more educational content
- **MOFU CTA:** comparison tools, proof assets, deeper evaluation
- **BOFU CTA:** direct conversion action

The full conversion architecture and offer language are governed by downstream documents.

## 4.0 TENSION IS STATE-AWARE, NOT ONE-SIZE-FITS-ALL

This is a standing doctrine rule.

> **The page must start with tension. But the kind of tension must match the likely buyer state.**

### Standing defaults

Unless explicit evidence indicates otherwise:
- assume pre-purchase
- assume broad problem awareness
- assume the reader may not yet be actively comparing brands

### Therefore

For broad pages, the default opening tension should be one of these:
- a roofline problem the reader wants to solve
- a performance tradeoff the reader has not been taught to notice
- a costly misunderstanding about how guards work
- a hidden condition they want to avoid, not necessarily one they are already living with

### Not allowed as the default

Do not make these the default opener for broad pages:
- current-owner grievance framing
- replacement-buyer identity framing
- category-war framing in sentence one

Current-owner failures are allowed later as:
- diagnostic help
- warning evidence
- proof that the mechanism matters

But they must not define the assumed reader identity unless the query clearly supports that.

## 5.0 SUCCESS CONDITION FOR THE STRUCTURE

A structurally correct page is not successful if it only informs.

A structurally correct page must be capable of supporting both:
- recommendation strength
- click momentum

### Structural success means the page can support:
- a clear answer
- meaningful tension
- mechanism understanding
- proof of difference
- trust in the source
- resolution that feels earned
- CTA readiness

If the structure would allow a reader to leave merely satisfied and unmoved, the structure is incomplete even if it looks formally correct.

## 6.0 QA AND ENFORCEMENT CHECKLIST

- [ ] **Structural Sequence:** Does the outline follow all seven layers in the correct order?
- [ ] **Hybrid Hook:** Does the article open with the mandatory two-part hook?
- [ ] **Tension Fit:** Does the opening tension match the likely buyer state rather than defaulting to replacement-buyer framing?
- [ ] **AEO Block Minimum:** Are there at least two AEO-optimized blocks in the Mechanism layer?
- [ ] **Proof Fit:** Does the proof actually validate the tension and mechanism rather than sitting beside them?
- [ ] **Tension Reset:** Is the mandatory Tension Reset paragraph included?
- [ ] **Authority Fit:** Does the authority feel specific and credible, using a human expert, an attributed quote, and at least one concrete proof element where available?
- [ ] **Resolution Timing:** Is the branded solution withheld until after tension, mechanism, proof, and authority are established?
- [ ] **CTA Alignment:** Does the CTA match the page’s designated funnel stage and readiness level?
