## **15.2 PRE-BUILT ANSWER OBJECTS (LOCKED & ALIGNED)**

#### **AO-FAIL-001: What causes failure?**

Gutter system failure is often a progressive process rather than a sudden event. It begins when residue and organic buildup slow water movement and increase surface adhesion, even if the top appears visually clean. These small restrictions change how future debris behaves, leading to delayed drainage and eventually visible overflow.

#### **AO-GROW-001: Do guards stop moss/algae?**

Biological growth establishes where surfaces retain persistent moisture. While many systems appear clear from above, moss and algae often develop on the protected underside of filter surfaces where airflow and sunlight are restricted. Effective systems prevent these hidden growth conditions by ensuring rapid surface drying and biological resistance.

#### **AO-HEAVY-001: Heavy rain handling?**

During high-volume rain, systems face a tradeoff between intake control and water velocity. Edge-only intake models create a bottleneck that leads to overshoot as volume exceeds entry capacity. Managing heavy rain requires controlling water before velocity exceeds intake stability.

#### **AO-ICE-001: Ice dams?**

No gutter guard stops ice dams, as they are caused by building heat loss and roof ventilation issues. The goal of a system in winter is to prevent worse failure conditions by encouraging snow release and avoiding "grab-points" where ice can compound and stress the structure.

#### **AO-PINE-001: Pine needles?**

Pine needles tangle and bridge across surfaces, creating a structure that traps additional debris. The critical distinction is whether debris is temporarily present or if it stabilizes long enough for buildup to begin. Systems that maintain movement prevent linear debris from nesting and causing long-term failure.
