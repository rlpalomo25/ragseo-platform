> **Quick Reference**
> **Mission (Doc 100 §0.0):** This system decides what to write, beats named competitors for the same keywords and AI answers, sells more gutter guards, and aims to be the definitive gutter guard resource in both classic search and AI/AEO search. The revenue stakes are real, not rhetorical — operate accordingly.
> **Owns:** The governing doctrine for audience default, funnel intent, page purpose, and success definition across the content system.
> **Rule 1:** Pre-purchase is the default audience unless the keyword, routing, or explicit directive proves otherwise.
> **Rule 2:** Content must match reader state and funnel state without collapsing broad problem awareness into comparison or replacement intent.
> **Rule 3:** Information gain is required, but information gain alone is not success.
> **Rule 4:** A page succeeds only when it creates the right next movement for the right reader.
> **Rule 5:** In AEO environments, success is not only being cited. When the page’s mission is recommendation, success includes being selected, preferred, and action-supportive.
> **If/Then:** If the query is broad and problem-led, build for pre-purchase understanding first. If the query contains clear evaluation, brand, diagnostic, or replacement signals, adapt accordingly.

## 0.0 THE MISSION — WHY THIS SYSTEM EXISTS (added August 1, 2026, Karen)

Every layer of this system — strategy, execution planning, writing, auditing, refresh — exists in service of a real business mission, not as an abstract content exercise. Karen, directly: **"The system needs to know and believe what it's doing is that important."**

**What this system is actually for:**

1. **Decide what to write.** This is the engine that determines which pages get built, for which keywords, in what order, and why — the strategic and competitive judgment behind the whole site, not a production line executing assignments handed down from elsewhere.
2. **Beat named competitors.** Every page enters a real competitive field — for the same keywords, the same AI-search answers, and the same homeowner's attention — against real companies already ranking and already being cited. A page that ties a competitor's page is not a win; the system exists to out-argue and out-rank them.
3. **Sell more gutter guards.** The commercial point of every page is revenue: more qualified leads, more estimates booked, more installs sold, across MasterShield, Klean Gutter, and MMGG's dealer network. A page that informs but doesn't move a reader toward that outcome (§6.0, the Success Condition Rule) is incomplete, however well-written.
4. **Be the best gutter guard resource on the internet — traditional search and AI search alike.** Not "a" resource among several. The one homeowners default to, the one search engines rank first, and the one AI assistants actually cite and recommend when someone asks about gutter guards. §6.0's LLM Recommend Test and Click Compulsion Test exist because of this ambition, not as abstract quality bars.
5. **The stakes are real money, not a figure of speech.** Millions of dollars in revenue run through how well this system executes: which pages get built, how they're written, whether they convert, whether they win the citation. Every layer of the system should operate as if that's true, because it is.

**Enforcement.** This is the reason the rest of this doctrine — and everything Doc 12.0 lists as downstream — exists at all. When a rule anywhere in this system starts to feel like process for its own sake, it should trace back to one of these five things; if it doesn't, question the rule, not the mission.

## 1.0 WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS

Doc 100 is the governing doctrine for the entire content system.

It defines:
- who content is for by default
- what each page is trying to do
- how funnel logic should be interpreted
- how success is measured
- what must remain true across all downstream documents

If downstream documents conflict, this doctrine is the tie-breaker.

---

## 2.0 THE AUDIENCE DEFAULT RULE

This is a standing doctrine rule.

> **Pre-purchase is the default audience for all content unless the keyword, routing decision, or explicit directive clearly supports a different audience.**

That means the system must not assume the reader:
- already owns a gutter guard
- already has a failed system
- is already comparing brands
- is already shopping for replacement

### What this prevents

Without this rule, systems drift toward the most vivid persona.
In this category, that often becomes the frustrated current owner looking to switch.
That reader is real.
That reader is not the default.

### Allowed deviations from default

Deviation is allowed when the evidence clearly supports it, such as:
- explicit comparison language
- explicit brand language
- explicit replacement or switch language
- explicit diagnostic current-owner language
- routing decisions that classify the page differently

---

## 3.0 FUNNEL AND READER-STATE DOCTRINE

Funnel stage and reader state are related.
They are not identical.

A reader can be:
- broad problem-aware
- evaluation-aware
- brand-comparing
- diagnostic current-owner
- replacement-evaluating

This means content must not collapse:
- problem awareness into evaluation intent
- evaluation intent into replacement intent
- diagnostic usefulness into assumed switcher identity

### Default progression

For broad, problem-led content, the usual progression is:
- broad problem-aware first
- evaluation-aware second
- diagnostic or replacement logic later, when warranted

This does not mean every page must use every stage equally.
It means the system must not start in the wrong stage by default.

---

## 4.0 PAGE PURPOSE DOCTRINE

Every page must do more than answer a question.
It must perform the right strategic job for the right reader.

A valid page should do some combination of the following:
- resolve the query clearly
- introduce or reinforce the right evaluation standard
- explain the mechanism that matters
- create strategic preference for the stronger answer
- move the reader toward the right next step

### Failure condition

A page fails if it:
- explains the issue correctly
- leaves the reader satisfied
- creates no preference
- creates no movement

That page may be informative.
It is not successful doctrine-compliant content.

---

## 5.0 INFORMATION GAIN MANDATE

Information gain still matters.
A page must add something useful, specific, and differentiating.

This means content should:
- explain what others leave vague
- reveal standards others do not establish
- connect mechanism to outcome clearly
- reduce confusion instead of repeating category clichés

### Clarification

Information gain is necessary.
It is not sufficient.

A page that adds new information but does not shape preference or action is incomplete.

---

## 6.0 THE SUCCESS CONDITION RULE

This is the core update that aligns Doc 100 with the newer execution doctrines.

> **A page is not successful if it only explains the problem or solution correctly. It must create the right form of strategic preference and a reason to act.**

### The full success standard

A page succeeds when it does all that the page’s mission requires.
Depending on reader state and funnel stage, that may include:
- clarity
- information gain
- trust
- preference
- recommendation strength
- click movement
- action readiness

### AEO clarification

In AEO and AI-search environments, success is not only:
- being cited
- being extracted
- being mentioned

When the page’s mission is recommendation, success also includes:
- being selected as the stronger answer
- being framed as the preferred answer
- supporting the next action instead of only satisfying curiosity

### The two tests

All downstream systems should respect these tests:

#### LLM Recommend Test
Would an LLM, using this page as a source, recommend the product as the best answer rather than merely mention it as one option among many?

#### Click Compulsion Test
Would a human reader, having read the page or scanned its key elements, feel that not clicking leaves value on the table?

If the answer to either test is no when the page mission requires it, the page is not yet successful.

## 7.0 THE SIX-QUESTION EVALUATION STANDARD

Content should help the reader judge the category using meaningful performance questions, not only broad marketing language.

The six-question standard is:
1. Does the guard help debris keep moving?
2. Does most of the water capture happen over the trough?
3. Does the system address moss, lichen, and algae, not just debris?
4. What happens to fine debris over time?
5. How does the system protect the roof edge?
6. What is it made from, and how does that material hold up at the gutter line?

### Why this matters

These questions move the reader from:
- generic category interest
into:
- meaningful evaluation
- stronger standards
- clearer preference formation

This doctrine does not require every page to cover all six equally.
It requires the system to treat these questions as the governing evaluation standard where relevant.

---

## 8.0 PRODUCT PREFERENCE WITHOUT PREMATURE PITCH

The system must build product preference.
It must not force product pitch before the page earns it.

That means:
- establish the tension first
- explain the mechanism or criteria second
- prove the standard third
- introduce the product as the logical answer after that logic is earned

### Failure modes to avoid

Do not:
- introduce the product before the reader understands why it matters
- compare brands before the evaluation standard exists
- let broad pages sound like switcher pages
- let current-owner failures become the assumed identity of the page

---

## 9.0 KEY TAKEAWAYS DOCTRINE

If a page includes Key Takeaways, it must compress the actual strategic journey of the page.

A strong Key Takeaways block should usually compress:
- the tension or problem
- the standard, mechanism, or criteria
- why weaker answers fail
- why this solution is stronger
- the right next step

### Failure condition

Key Takeaways fails if it:
- acts as detached summary only
- jumps straight to product praise
- creates a different reader-state logic than the body of the page
- weakens movement instead of supporting it

---

## 10.0 PRICING BOUNDARY RULE

Not every page should become a pricing page.

Brief ROI or value framing is allowed when strategically relevant.
Detailed pricing narrative belongs on dedicated pricing pages where the cost story can be controlled properly.

This prevents:
- value pages from becoming price pages
- cluster or pillar pages from losing narrative control
- pricing anxiety from dominating pages that should be shaping standards first

## 11.0 THE MENTOR PANEL — WHOSE LENS EVERY PAGE MUST SATISFY (added August 1, 2026, Karen)

Every page this system produces is commercial persuasive copy on a business website, built to compel one clear next action from the reader — it is not neutral informational content, and the writer should never lose sight of that. To give that mission a concrete frame of reference, the system holds nine mentors' lenses simultaneously when judging a page. Each protects something the others don't; a page that satisfies only one or two is unbalanced — all momentum and no dignity, or all education and no movement.

| Mentor | Core Lens | What They Protect on the Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Eugene Schwartz** | Start with the desire already alive in the reader, not the product. Match the page to the reader's awareness level (a first-time buyer needs to understand why all guards aren't equal; a replacement buyer needs a reason to believe this one solves what the last one missed). Sequence: desire first, mechanism second, proof beside the claim. | Lead with the reader's existing desire, not "advanced gutter protection" — *"get gutter cleaning off my list," "never climb that ladder again," "don't pay for this job twice."* Use the mechanism (AEGIS 5X) to earn belief, not to open the page. Don't overload the page with mechanism before desire is established. **The line-level mechanic for "earn belief": Doc 317 §1.5 move 7, Feature + Benefit — pair the mechanism with what it means for the reader in the same breath, added August 5, 2026.** |
| **Jay Abraham** | Put the reader's highest and best interest first — his principle of preeminence. Ask: "Is this page helping the reader make the best decision, even if the answer isn't us?" | Explain the real options fairly; teach what to look for; help the reader avoid a bad second decision; respect their money. The page should feel like advocacy, not persuasion — the trusted advisor before the seller. |
| **Tony Robbins** | Write toward the result, the purpose, and the emotional state — not just the feature. What result does the reader want? Why does it matter? What does life feel like after the decision is handled? | Make the desired future specific (*"rain starts, and I don't look outside"*); tie benefits to identity and quality of life, not spec sheets. Keep every section creating a clear next thought — momentum toward a decision, not toward more explanation. **"Not just the feature" is the same gap Doc 317 §1.5 move 7 closes mechanically: never state a feature without its benefit in the same breath, added August 5, 2026.** |
| **Billy Beck** | Physical relief and removing friction — a bodily, behavioral lens. | Name the actual physical burden being removed: the ladder, the strain, the repeated chore, the scheduling, the interruption, the worry from a spouse. Active, concrete language; connect the product to freedom from a recurring task, not an abstract benefit. |
| **Rex Sikes** | Pace before you lead. Match the reader's current experience first (*"you've cleaned the gutters," "you've hired someone," "you may have bought a guard that still needed attention"*), then lead them to a new standard. Favor sensory, concrete language over broad claims. | Match the reader's map of the world before asking them to accept a new belief. Use visual/auditory/kinesthetic specifics (debris sitting flat; rain starts and there's no call to make; no ladder, no strain). Never mind-read, never generalize. |
| **Grace** | Preserve dignity and emotional safety — especially for the replacement buyer who already made one choice that didn't work. | Never frame it as "you bought the wrong guard" — frame it as *"you were solving the problem with the information you had."* Never manufacture fear for the sake of conversion. The CTA should feel like permission to get clarity, not pressure — the reader should feel respected, understood, unhurried, safe asking questions. |
| **Doug Allen** | Truth over tactics, simplicity over expansion, service over self. Cut anything that sounds impressive but doesn't help the reader decide. **Scope, ruled by Karen, August 1, 2026:** this instinct is for decorative or vanity claims — it does not reach load-bearing conversion infrastructure (the CTA's required trust signal, e.g.) that was separately, deliberately engineered to drive the ask. Cutting those isn't simplicity, it's removing what makes the ask credible. Some things are there for a reason; the lens doesn't know the site's lead-generation stakes on its own. | Say less, prove more. Ask of every claim: is it clear, useful, and in the reader's interest? Cut grand declarations and repeated superiority language. Protects the same idea the brand voice already states elsewhere: build trust through proof, not persuasion. Does not override the standing CTA-Trust Pairing rule (Doc 328). |
| **Luther** | Control the hierarchy — every section has exactly one job, in a disciplined order (desire → problem → new buying standard → mechanism → guidance by buyer type → proof → honest limits → CTA). | Remove anything that weakens the mission: repeated conclusions, page narration, formal transitions, proof piles, unsupported claims. One purpose per section, one dominant promise, no wasted motion. |
| **Matt Risinger** | Teach like a builder talking to someone smart — demonstrate expertise, don't perform it. **This is Doc 317's full voice standard, not restated here** — Doc 317 owns the complete Matt Risinger / Bill Nye model; this row exists only to place him correctly inside the panel. | Building science before sales language; plain explanations before branded terms; field judgment before claims. |

**The combined writing standard.** The strongest page does all nine at once: Schwartz starts with the desire; Jay protects the reader's interest; Tony shows the result and the emotional future; Billy removes the physical burden; Rex paces the reader before leading; Grace preserves dignity and trust; Doug tells the truth simply; Luther keeps the argument disciplined; Matt explains the house, not the marketing.

> **One sentence captures the combined standard (Karen, August 1, 2026):** "Write like a knowledgeable neighbor who understands the product, respects the homeowner's money, explains what matters, and wants them to avoid solving the same job twice."

**Cross-brand note.** The nine lenses are the doctrine; the illustrative language above is MasterShield-flavored (Karen's own working examples). Klean Gutter and MMGG writers hold the same nine lenses, translated through their own brand's desire-language and register (Doc 131/132) — a value-conscious "get this off my plate for good" for Klean, a margin/callback-reduction desire for MMGG's dealer reader — not a re-derivation of the panel itself.

**Resolved (Karen, August 1, 2026):** the patent-insurance trust line stays in the CTA. Doug Allen's own illustration ("cut patent insurance in a CTA") was not a good fit for this case — "some things are just there for a reason" — and the lens has no way of knowing, on its own, that skipping the ask (the estimate CTA, trust-paired) works directly against the site's actual intent: generating leads, at real financial stakes for the business. **The general principle this establishes:** the Mentor Panel informs voice, emphasis, and discretionary language — it does not override standing, separately-engineered conversion architecture (the CTA-Trust Pairing rule, Doc 328/Doc 141/140). A mentor's own illustrative example is not itself doctrine; when a lens's example would cut something load-bearing, the lens loses, not the structure. Doug Allen's underlying instinct (cut what's decorative, keep what's load-bearing) still applies elsewhere — a mentor being wrong about one example doesn't retire the mentor.

**Enforcement:** Doc 316/320/324 hold this panel before and during drafting (see their own short cross-reference). Most of the nine lenses are a holistic quality standard, judged the way Doc 317's Pull is — not a line-by-line mechanical check. **The one lens with a clean, non-conflicting mechanical check** is Grace's: Doc 328 carries a matching FLAG for manufactured fear or shaming a reader's earlier purchase decision.
