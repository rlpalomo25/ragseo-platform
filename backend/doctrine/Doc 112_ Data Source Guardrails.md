## 1.0 Purpose & Scope

This document is the definitive guide to what data sources we can use in our content. It protects our credibility by ensuring every statistic, claim, and data point stands up to scrutiny.

**The Golden Rule:** If a statistic appears in your content, it must meet the standards outlined below and be sourced from the **Citation Resource Bank (Doc 113)**. The quality of sources directly impacts performance metrics as measured by **Doc 201 (Content Performance Rubric)**. No exceptions.

---

## 2.0 Understanding Source Authority Levels

To avoid confusion with the Doctrine Tier system (Doc 225), source quality is now measured in **Authority Levels**.

### Authority Level 1 (AL1) — Highest Authority

Gold-standard sources. Independently verifiable and professionally maintained.

| Source Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Government agencies | NOAA, EPA, USDA, US Census |
| Academic institutions | University studies, peer-reviewed journals |
| Independent testing orgs | ASTM International, UL |

### Authority Level 2 (AL2) — Acceptable with Framing

Reliable, but require nuanced language (e.g., "According to the NRCA...").

| Source Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Industry associations | NRCA, NAHB |
| Established trade publications | Remodeling Magazine, This Old House, Angi |

### Authority Level 3 (AL3) — Use for Context Only

These sources require independent verification from an AL1 or AL2 source before any claim can be used. If you cannot find confirmation, discard the claim.

| Source Type | Acceptable Use |
|---|---|
| General news articles | Background context only |
| Competitor websites | Identifying claims to contrast |

## 3.0 The AI-Preferred Source Rule

To maximize our content’s visibility within AI systems, we prioritize sources that AI models are already citing.

> **Source Selection Priority:** When choosing between two sources of equal authority, give preference to the one that has already been cited in a Google AI Overview for a related keyword.

**Enforcement Rule:** An AI-preferred source is not an automatic exception to our quality standards. Before it can be used, it **must** be vetted, approved, and added to the **Citation Resource Bank (Doc 113)**. This ensures that even AI-driven signals are filtered through our human-led quality control.

---

## 4.0 Clarification on AI Usage

There is a critical distinction between using AI as a source and using it as a signal.

*   **AI as a Source (Prohibited):** You must **never** cite a statistic, fact, or claim that is generated by an AI model (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini). This is considered an invented source and is a violation of our credibility standards.
*   **AI as a Signal (Encouraged):** You **should** analyze the citation patterns of AI Overviews to identify which third-party sources the AI already trusts. This is a powerful intelligence signal, identified by the AI Landscape Monitor, that informs our own source selection.
