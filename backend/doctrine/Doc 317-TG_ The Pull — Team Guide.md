*What makes a page sing, and how to do it on purpose.*

---

## What "the Pull" is

The Pull is the quality that makes a reader want the next sentence. When a page has it, you don't *study* the page — you're *carried* by it. You glide. You finish a section and you're already leaning into the next one.

We saw it first on the AEGIS 5X pillar page. The whole team read it and said the same thing: *give us more.* That reaction is the Pull. The goal now is to stop treating it like a happy accident and start doing it on purpose, on every page.

Here's the test, and it's the only one that matters: **read a section out loud.** If you naturally lean in for the next sentence, the Pull is working. If you have to slow down and re-read to hold the meaning, it isn't — even if every fact on the page is correct.

---

## The seven moves that create it

The Pull isn't magic. It's seven repeatable moves. Use them on purpose.

**1. The two-beat turn.** A statement, then a short reframe.
> "That's the promise. It's also the limit."
> "That's not a debris problem. That's a water problem."
> "The system doesn't change. The rain does."

It's how a confident person actually talks. Every turn is a little hit of momentum.

**2. One idea per paragraph — and keep paragraphs short.** Ideas arrive single file. Never make the reader hold five clauses in their head at once. The white space *is* the easy listening.

**3. Feeling first, engineering second.** Open on what the homeowner *sees, fears, or feels* — then let the engineering serve the feeling.
> "You know the feeling. It's raining hard. It's late. And somewhere in the back of your mind you're thinking about the gutters."

The mechanism is in the room. It's just never the host.

**4. Talk to the reader, in the present tense.** Second person. Direct. The occasional nudge.
> "Don't ask 'does it work?' Ask how it works."

It's a conversation, not a spec sheet.

**5. Make music with parallelism.** Triads and matched fragments stick in the ear.
> "Five failure points. Five engineered responses. One system."
> "No prying. No pressure. No damage."

**6. Calm certainty, plain words.** No hedging, no jargon stacks. The knowledgeable neighbor who's seen it all and isn't impressed by marketing. State the verdict. Don't qualify it to death.

**7. Feature, then its benefit — never one without the other.** Every claim pairs the two in the same breath: what it is, then what that actually means for the reader.
> "CopperCare weaves copper into the mesh [feature], so what's collecting under it never gets the chance to grow into a bigger problem [benefit]."

A feature alone reads like a spec sheet. A benefit alone reads like a promise with nothing behind it. Paired, the reader's internal voice says *yes* — and a page is a string of those, not one big pitch at the end.

## A quick before-and-after

**Stop-and-study (no Pull):**
> Self-cleaning is produced by a combination of surface geometry, lateral water velocity, and aperture-level turbulence, which together ensure that debris of varying particle sizes does not stabilize on the filter surface over time.

**The Pull:**
> Self-cleaning comes down to one idea you can watch happen. Moving water carries the small stuff. Still water leaves it behind. Pitch the guard with your roof, and the rain does the work.

Same facts. One makes you concentrate. The other carries you.

## What breaks the Pull

- Long sentences with stacked clauses.
- A section that opens with a *mechanism* or a *definition* instead of a *feeling*.
- Three technical paragraphs in a row with no turn, no scene, no parallel.
- Hedged, over-qualified verdicts.

When a stretch of a page makes the reader *concentrate* instead of *coast*, the Pull is gone.

---

## One myth to kill

"The deep-dive pages are technical, so they can't have the Pull."

Not true. The technical pages carry real density — that's their job. The Pull doesn't mean dumbing the mechanism down. It means **layering these seven moves over the technical content** so the main line stays easy listening, and the heaviest detail lives in an optional/expandable spot for the readers who want it. The pillar earns the read; the deep-dive rewards it. Both should pull.

---

## How we use it

- **Writers:** build the seven moves in as you draft. Open every section feeling-first. Read it aloud before you hand it off.
- **Reviewers:** check for the Pull the same way — read aloud, and flag any stretch that makes you slow down.
- **Where it's written down:** the full standard lives in Doc 317 (Section 1.5, "The Pull"). The writer agent applies it; the auditor checks for it.
