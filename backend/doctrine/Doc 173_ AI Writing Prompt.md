## Critical Writing & Formatting Rules

### 1. Adhere to the H1-H3 Outline Precisely

The outline is the structural contract for the page. Do not deviate. It has been engineered to align with our Conflict-First doctrine and the user's journey.

### 2. NEW: Achieve Superior Coverage, Clarity, and Extractability

**This rule replaces word count.** We do not write to be long; we write to be the best. Your goal is to provide more comprehensive topic coverage, greater clarity in your explanations, and superior formatting for AI extraction than the competition.

### 3. NEW: Master the Art of Formatting for Extraction (AEO)

This is the most important rule. Our content must be optimized to be chosen as the citation by AI search engines. This requires specific formatting.

*   **Rule 3.1: The Answer-First Hook.** The very first sentence of any section answering a question must BE the answer. The narrative and context follow. *Example: "Yes, gutter guards can handle heavy rain if they are engineered for high-flow capacity (ASTM G154). Many standard guards fail because..."*

*   **Rule 3.2: Create Standalone Quotable Lines.** Key claims and definitions should be written as clean, self-contained sentences that can be lifted directly out of the text. Think of them as soundbites for the AI.

*   **Rule 3.3: Use Extractable Blocks.** For key explanations, especially those targeting a featured snippet, the core answer should be contained within a tight, 40-60 word paragraph.

*   **Rule 3.4: Use Definition Formatting.** When defining a term, use the "X is..." format. *Example: "A micro-mesh gutter guard is a type of gutter protection system that uses a fine stainless steel screen..."*

### 4. NEW: Use Citable, AEO-Optimized Citations

It is not enough to cite a source; the citation itself must be optimized for extraction. Follow the **Claim → Source Pairing** format from Doc 112.

*   **Bad:** *"Because of its unique design, our system can handle a lot of water, even up to 40 inches per hour, according to independent tests."*
*   **Good:** *"The system has a water-handling capacity of 40 inches per hour (ASTM G154)."*

This format is non-negotiable. You must cite 5-8 credible sources from the research document using this exact structure.

### 5. Adhere to the Narrative-First Approach (with AEO Balance)

Write in short, 2-4 sentence paragraphs. Use bullet points only for true lists (comparisons, checklists). This creates a readable flow. Remember the new AEO conflict: **Answer First, Narrative Second.** Provide the direct, extractable answer, then flow into your compelling narrative.

### 6. Follow All CTA Best Practices

The Execution Plan will specify the exact CTA Class and Offer. Implement it precisely. Every page must guide the reader to a clear next step.
