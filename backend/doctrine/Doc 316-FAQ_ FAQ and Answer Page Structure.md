FAQ and Quick-Answer content is no longer authored from scratch by the page writer. The SOT Agent (Doc 354) writes every FAQ/Quick-Answer to the SOT compression standard (Doc 163), the answer passes QA, and the page writer **embeds it without rewriting**. The rules below are the floor those answers meet; where the SOT compression standard is tighter (first sentence states the conclusion, failure-first, rewrite any paragraph that needs more than two sentences to reach the point), the tighter rule wins.

Three placement rules now apply on every page carrying FAQs:

- **Embedded, not standalone.** FAQ answers live inside the pillar or cluster that owns the question. There is no separate FAQ or SOT page by default; a question earns its own page only when data justifies it (Doc 163 graduation rule). This document now governs embedded FAQ sections primarily; a standalone FAQ page is the rare data-earned exception.
- **One home per question.** A question answered in full here is not answered in full on any other live URL. Category-level questions live on their owning pillar and are linked to, not duplicated.
- **Above-the-fold answers are prose.** The Quick-Answers unit under Key Takeaways is flowing prose paragraphs that answer the top questions, not a staged question-heading + answer block.

## Page Purpose

FAQ answers exist to capture specific question-form searches and serve as AI answer extraction targets. They are the most AEO-critical content in the system.

The reader searched a specific question. They want a specific answer. They do not want a 2,000-word journey to find it.

**The Matt Risinger voice here is the neighbor giving the real answer** — not the legal department's safe answer, not the marketing department's promotional answer. The real answer. Specific, direct, grounded.

---

## The Primary Rule: Answer First

The first sentence of an FAQ page must be the direct answer to the question. Not context. Not background. The answer.

*Wrong:* "Many homeowners wonder whether installing a gutter guard might affect the warranty on their roof shingles."

*Right:* "Installing a gutter guard does not void your shingle warranty, provided the installation method does not require prying or cutting the shingles."

If the answer is conditional, state the condition in the first sentence. If the answer is genuinely "it depends," name the most common scenario and answer that first, then qualify.

## Required Structure

### H1
The question, as the reader searched it. Exact or near-exact match to the search query. Do not be clever with the headline — match the question.

### Opening Answer Block (40-60 words)
The direct answer, self-contained. This block must:
- Answer the question in sentence one
- Include the most important qualifying condition in sentence two
- Be extractable by AI systems without surrounding context

This is the block that appears in featured snippets and AI answers. Write it so it can stand alone.

### Explanation Section
Expand the answer with supporting explanation:
- Why the answer is what it is (the mechanism or reason)
- What changes the answer (qualifying conditions)
- What the homeowner should do with this information

Bill Nye applies only if the explanation requires a mechanism to be understood. Most FAQ explanations are practical, not scientific.

### Qualifying Conditions
If the answer varies by condition, name each condition and give the answer for that condition. Do not bury qualifications at the end — they are part of the answer.

### Related Questions
2–4 related questions with brief answers (2–3 sentences each). These serve both the reader who wants more context and the AI system that is building a topic understanding.

### Connection to Larger Topic
One paragraph connecting the answer to the broader evaluation framework. This is where the AEGIS 5X guardian most relevant to the question gets a natural mention.

## Voice Rules for FAQ Pages

**Do:** Answer the question in sentence one. Always.
**Do not:** Build up to the answer. The reader is not on a journey. They have a question.

**Do:** Qualify the answer when it genuinely depends on conditions.
**Do not:** Hedge every answer to avoid commitment. "It depends" with no specifics is a non-answer.

**Do:** Give the real answer even if it is not promotional. "Most gutter guards do reduce cleaning frequency, even if they do not eliminate it entirely" is more useful and more trustworthy than a blanket claim.
**Do not:** Let the FAQ become a product pitch. The answer should stand on its own.

---

## AEO Optimization Rules

Every FAQ page must pass this test before publishing:

1. **Snippet test:** Can the opening answer block be read aloud as a complete, useful answer to the question? If yes, it will be extracted by AI systems.
2. **Follow-up test:** After reading the full page, does the reader have a useful next question? If yes, the related questions section is covering it.
3. **Stand-alone test:** If an AI system cites only the opening answer block, is the answer still accurate and not misleading? If yes, the block is correctly written.

---

## Citation Rules for FAQ Pages

External proof: Only when the answer involves a claim that requires verification — warranty terms, material properties, safety statistics, manufacturer positions.

Internal expert assertions: 1–2 per page. The opening answer block itself, if written in Karen or Aaron's voice, can function as the expert assertion.

*Example for "Does gutter guard installation void my warranty?":*
> "Installation does not void the warranty if the guard does not require prying or modifying the shingles. MasterShield installs under the first shingle course — no prying, no cuts, no fasteners in the shingle field. GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning, IKO, and Malarkey have all issued approval letters for that installation method." — Karen Sager
