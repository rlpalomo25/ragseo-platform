### 4.5.1 Field Stories, Case Studies & Real Installs — Brand-Locked (added July 22, 2026, per Karen)

**A real field story is a fact, not reusable creative material.** A specific customer job, install, or case study (e.g., "a Connecticut homeowner was about to spend $30,000 on French drains before [brand] fixed the overflow") happened under one specific brand. That brand-lock is a fact like a patent holder or a warranty rate — it does not move because a sibling page needs proof.

**Rule:** A brand-owned field story may be reused across **multiple pages of that same brand** (e.g., a MasterShield story can appear on several MasterShield pages). It must **never be reattributed to a different brand in the portfolio**, even though MasterShield, Klean Gutter, and MMGG share ownership, engineering, and personnel. Rewriting "put MasterShield on first" as "put Klean Gutter on first" for a sibling page asserts an installation that did not happen under that brand — this is a factual misstatement, not a style or duplicate-content issue, and fails QA regardless of how well the rest of the page reads.

**If a sibling brand needs proof and has no real story of its own for that scenario:** genericize (describe the scenario without naming an installer brand) rather than reattribute. Do not invent a new "real" story for the other brand to fill the gap. A different, verified real story specific to that brand is always preferable if one exists — ask Karen before assuming there isn't one.

**Exception:** MMGG (the manufacturer) may reference a MasterShield installation as an example of AEGIS 5X in action, since MMGG is explicitly positioned as reporting on both consumer brands' real-world use (Doc 132 §2.4) — this is different from Klean or MasterShield claiming each other's jobs as their own.

**First caught:** July 22, 2026, on the Klean Gutter "gutter guard problems" draft, where the MasterShield $30K French-drain story (a real install, Doc 435) had been rewritten with "put Klean Gutter on first." Fixed by genericizing on the Klean draft.

### 4.5.2 Named Internal Quotes (Karen Sager / Aaron Kapfer) — Person-Locked, Never Blended (added July 22, 2026, per Karen)

**A quote attributed to a named person must be that person's actual words.** If a quote is attributed to Karen Sager, it stays Karen's; if attributed to Aaron Kapfer, it stays Aaron's. Do not:
- Reassign a real quote's speaker to match a page's default-author convention (e.g., Klean defaults to Aaron as primary author — that default governs the byline, not who gets credited for someone else's observation).
- Blend one person's observation into another person's attributed quote to make a page's callout requirement (Doc 194 From the Field) easier to satisfy.
- Tighten, combine, or extend field notes into a fuller-sounding quote and present it as verbatim.

**Verbatim confirmation is required before ship (ruled by Karen, July 22, 2026) — this is not optional and not a judgment call.** Every From the Field quote (Doc 194) must be confirmed as the named person's actual words before a page publishes. A writer may draft with a tightened/combined version as a placeholder, marked `[paraphrase - unconfirmed]` in the working file, but **that marker must be resolved — confirmed verbatim or corrected — before handoff passes Doc 328/Doc 329.** A page with an unresolved `[paraphrase - unconfirmed]` quote is a hard FAIL, same weight as a missing quote entirely; it is not a lower-priority nice-to-have.

**Concept reuse across brands is fine; the person's exact words are not automatically portable.** The same underlying design philosophy (e.g., "a guard should never force the shingle to move") can inform quotes on multiple brand pages, but each page's quote must be either (a) the same real quote reused on multiple pages of the **same** brand, or (b) a freshly composed, distinctly worded quote for the other brand — confirmed with the actual speaker, not a reworded version of the first brand's quote passed off as a new one.

- **Shared corporate facts (all brands — MasterShield, Klean, MMGG):** feet installed (10M+), homes protected (75,000+), and the IPISC-insured patent portfolio (17+).
