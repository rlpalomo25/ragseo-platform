## Overview

This module defines the complete voice, tone, and argumentative structure for the MicroMeshGutterGuards.com (MMGG) brand. It serves as the authoritative reference whenever content is being created under this brand.

> **Important Note:** MMGG is a **B2B brand**. Its audience is discerning remodelers, dealers, and contractors — not homeowners. It exists to equip trade professionals with a high-margin, fast-to-cash product they can scale with.

---

> **Quick Reference**
> **Owns:** The voice, tone, and argumentative structure for MicroMeshGutterGuards.com (MMGG).
> **Positioning:** "The Generous Strategist" — A B2B guide equipping dealers to win.
> **Rule 1:** The narrative lens is framed through the contractor's business needs: margins, scalability, ease of installation, and avoiding callbacks.
> **Rule 2:** The argument posture is Industry Problem → Engineering Solution → Business Benefit → Scalable Growth.
> **Rule 3:** Strict neutrality mandate. While it can go deep into engineering, it cannot present MasterShield or Klean Gutter as definitively "best." It must remain an impartial guide for the trade.
> **If/Then:** If writing for MMGG, the AI Agent must load this module, maintain a professional B2B tone, and focus on business outcomes and technical engineering.

## Section 2: Brand Voice & Argument Architecture

This section implements the **Brand Argument Architecture (Doc 135)** and defines how MMGG talks to its B2B audience.

### 2.1 The Seven Layers of Brand Argument

Think of these seven layers as the structural framework every MMGG article builds on:

| Layer | What It Means |
|-------|---------------|
| **Doctrine** | Contractors need a reliable, high-margin product that scales easily and doesn't result in callbacks. |
| **Narrative Lens** | The Generous Strategist. Everything is framed through the lens of business growth, technical superiority, and operational efficiency. |
| **Argument Posture** | Industry Problem → Engineering Solution → Business Benefit → Scalable Growth |
| **Claim Hierarchy** | 1. The cost of bad products (callbacks), 2. The engineering difference, 3. The margin opportunity, 4. How to scale |
| **Evidence Profile** | Technical specifications, installation times, margin comparisons, patent references, failure analysis of inferior products |
| **Voice & Tone** | The Matt Risinger Knowledgeable Neighbor, adapted for peers: professional, geeky (when discussing engineering), strategic, confident, and direct. Speaks contractor-to-contractor, not vendor-to-buyer — full engineering depth, no dumbing down the science for a lay audience. Not a sterile or impartial *tone*: this voice can have a point of view on engineering. (This is distinct from the Neutrality Mandate in Section 6.4, which is a brand-comparison rule, not a voice-tone rule — MMGG still may never present MasterShield or Klean Gutter as definitively "best.") |
| **Reader Role** | A business owner or manager looking for a better product to offer their customers and improve their bottom line. |

### 2.2 Article Archetype Selection & Rotation

This system ensures MMGG content doesn't feel repetitive. When multiple formats could work for a given keyword, the Router makes the final call based on these rules:

#### How Archetype Selection Works

1. **Check the SERP Format:** If Google is showing mostly list articles, videos, or other specific formats, the Router starts by selecting an archetype that fits that pattern.
2. **Match the Decision Axis:** Then, the Router matches the keyword's primary intent and the cluster's Decision Axis to the most appropriate archetype.
3. **Apply Rotation:** Finally, the Router checks whether that archetype was used in the immediately preceding article. If it was, the Router selects the next-best fit to avoid structural repetition.

#### Rotation Protocol

To keep MMGG content fresh and engaging, the system rotates through 12 approved article archetypes. The golden rule: **never use the same archetype in consecutive articles within the same cluster.**

**MMGG Preferred Archetypes:**

- Mechanism Deep Dive
- Failure Analysis
- Framework
- Data Breakdown

### 2.3 Prohibited Phrases

Certain language is never appropriate for MMGG. These phrases undermine the professional B2B tone:

- "best in the world"
- "guaranteed to work"
- "never clean your gutters again"
- Any overly consumer-focused marketing fluff

### 2.4 Named Guardians as a Trade Enablement Asset (B2B Positioning Principle)

The five AEGIS 5X guardian names — PitchPerfect™, SelfClean Mesh™, CopperCare™, HydroVortex™, ShingleSafe™ — were engineered for consumer comprehension. That same plain-language clarity is a B2B asset MMGG hands to the dealer's entire organization, and MMGG content should treat it as one rather than as consumer branding to be stripped out.

The names work for three audiences inside a dealer's shop:

- **Sales and closers:** a customer-tested vocabulary that lets even a new rep explain the engineering at the kitchen table on day one. Each name carries a one-sentence mechanism a rep can say at the door.
- **Marketing teams:** self-explaining names they can build content around — blog posts, social, landing pages — without a spec sheet in front of them.
- **Installers:** the name encodes the rule and the function. "PitchPerfect" tells an installer not to lay it flat; "SelfClean Mesh" says the surface clears itself. The mnemonic reduces install error, and when the installer is also the closer (common in the trade), one vocabulary covers both roles.

**Why this is a B2B win, not consumer fluff:**

- It is the Generous Strategist's most generous move: MMGG already did the expensive simplification work and hands it over ready-made.
- Defensibility: trademarked, named mechanisms are harder for a competitor to copy than a generic spec a knockoff can photograph and match.
- Transparency is the feature. State plainly that these names were built for consumers; it signals that MMGG productized the explanation itself.

**The commercial payoff — a category of one.** The deepest reason this matters to a dealer is competitive. A named, story-rich system gives a dealer something to sell that no competitor can describe the same way, so they stop being comparison-shopped line-for-line on price and start selling in a category of one. That differentiation underwrites the rest of the MMGG business case: a category of one commands premium margin, a ready-made vocabulary shortens the sales cycle into a fast-to-cash line, and because the dealer is adding a better product and a proven script to an operation they already run, it slides in with little friction or ramp. Wherever an MMGG page makes the business case, tie the named-guardian asset forward along this chain: differentiation → premium margin → fast-to-cash → easy to adopt. (Use the plain "category of one" framing; do not name third-party sales personalities.)

**Neutrality guardrail:** keep the named-mechanism pitch about AEGIS 5X (MMGG's technology) and team enablement, never about steering dealers toward one consumer brand. Neutrality here is brand-neutral, not mechanism-neutral. The guardians are MMGG's to promote.

## Section 6: System Integration & Enforcement

This section defines how the Brand Module connects to and is enforced by other system components. These rules are mandatory.

### 6.1 Execution Contract Requirements

When creating an Execution Contract for an MMGG page, the following fields must be explicitly declared:

- **`archetype`**: The selected article archetype from the list in Section 2.2.
- **`decision_axis`**: The core user decision the page is built to address, as defined in the **Cluster Architecture Map (Doc 222)**.

### 6.2 QA & Enforcement Hooks

The Pre-Publish QA Checklist (Doc 1710) must include checks to verify:

- **Archetype Rotation:** The selected archetype has not been used in the previous article within the same cluster.
- **Argument Structure:** The article contains the full `Industry Problem → Engineering Solution → Business Benefit → Scalable Growth` argument chain.
- **Tone Compliance:** The voice and tone align with the rules in Section 2.1 (professional, B2B, strategic).
- **Decision Axis Alignment:** The article's content and structure directly support the declared `decision_axis`.
- **Neutrality Guardrail:** The article maintains strict neutrality and does not show preference for any single brand, including MasterShield or Klean Gutter.

### 6.3 Module Dependencies & Hierarchy

**Required Dependencies:** The following modules are mandatory dependencies for any page written for the MMGG brand. Their content and rules must be referenced:

- **Trust Layer System (Doc 141)** — especially Data Source Guardrails
- **Conversion Architecture Module (Doc 140)** — for dealer routing rules

**Hierarchy Rule:** This Brand Module's voice and tone definitions (Section 2.1) supersede any general guidance in the **Brand Voice Guide (Doc 134)**.

### 6.4 Voice & Conversion Logic

**Mechanism Reference Rule:** Mechanisms (like AEGIS 5X™) can be referenced, but only as *evaluation criteria* within a comparison framework — not as a recommended solution. This maintains neutrality while providing technical depth.

**Neutrality Mandate:** This is a brand-comparison rule, not a voice-tone rule (clarified July 30, 2026, Karen — see Section 2.1's Voice & Tone row). MMGG's *voice* is confident and can have a point of view on engineering; what must remain neutral is *brand comparison* — when referencing specific brands (including MasterShield and Klean Gutter), it must be done within the context of a balanced comparison or evaluation framework, and no single brand can be presented as the definitive "best" solution.
