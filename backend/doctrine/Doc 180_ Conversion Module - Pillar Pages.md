## 1.0 THE ROLE OF THE PILLAR PAGE

Pillar pages are the authoritative hubs of the Hub and Spoke Linking Topology. Their job is to capture broad, high-volume search traffic, establish the evaluation standard, build trust, build product preference, and distribute readers to the right next step.

Pillar pages are educational, comprehensive, and trust-building.
They are not passive encyclopedic assets.

### Correct role definition

A Pillar page should:
- answer the broad category question
- define the standards that matter
- build preference for the engineered solution
- route the reader to the strongest next step
- create momentum without outrunning reader readiness

The primary conversion goal is usually not an immediate sale.
The primary goal is movement:
- deeper evaluation
- qualified next-step action
- cluster click-through
- inspection or fit guidance where earned

---

## 2.0 THE CONVERSION PRINCIPLE

Pillar pages must create movement.

They must not:
- stop at education
- become neutral information repositories
- treat broad readers like they are already bottom-funnel
- end in satisfaction without action

### The governing rule

> **CTA pressure on Pillar pages must match reader state, page value, and what the page has actually earned.**

This means:
- early asks are usually light
- mid-page asks can become firmer after proof and criteria
- bottom CTA can be strong, but only if the page has earned it

### Page-value modifier

CTA firmness is shaped by both:
- reader state
- page value or intent strength

Reader state controls whether the ask is earned.
Page value controls how assertively the page should capitalize on an earned ask.

Examples of page-value signals:
- high commercial spillover
- strong cluster-routing importance
- high-value traffic
- high difficulty
- high conversion potential from the topic

These signals may justify firmer CTA language later in the page.
They do not justify premature hard-close behavior.

---

## 3.0 THE TWO REQUIRED TESTS

Every Pillar page must be able to pass both tests.

### 1. LLM Recommend Test
Would an LLM, using this page as a source, recommend the product as the best answer rather than merely mention it as one option among many?

A pillar page fails this test if:
- it explains the category but does not create product preference
- it presents the brand or system as generic
- it builds citation value without recommendation value

### 2. Click Compulsion Test
Would a human reader, having read this page, feel that not clicking leaves value on the table?

A pillar page fails this test if:
- the page satisfies curiosity but creates no movement
- every CTA is so soft that the page becomes a dead end
- the strongest CTA arrives without enough trust or preference
- the conclusion wraps up information instead of creating momentum

## 4.0 CTA PRESSURE BY READER STATE

CTA pressure is determined by reader state first, then adjusted by page value.

### A. Broad Problem-Aware Pillar Page
Typical state:
- reader is early
- reader is learning the category
- reader wants clarity before comparison

CTA gradient:
- top: soft navigational CTA
- after intro: soft or hybrid CTA
- middle: hybrid CTA
- bottom: hybrid or earned firm CTA

Best asks:
- jump to the section that fits your question
- see what actually matters before you buy
- compare the standards that separate stronger from weaker systems

Do not:
- hard-close above the fold by default
- speak as if the reader is already switching from a failed system

### B. Evaluation-Heavy Pillar Page
Typical state:
- reader is still broad enough for a pillar page
- but the topic has stronger comparison or decision energy
- page value is often higher

CTA gradient:
- top: soft or hybrid CTA
- middle: hybrid to firm CTA
- bottom: firm CTA

Best asks:
- compare the key options
- see what standard matters most
- move into the right cluster page, estimate path, or fit path

### Important note
Pillar pages are not standard replacement pages.
If replacement or diagnostic signals surface inside the traffic mix, the page may acknowledge them, but the page should still serve the broad entry audience first unless strategy says otherwise.

## 5.0 CTA PLACEMENT AND CADENCE

Pillar pages are usually long-form. CTA pacing must guide rather than overwhelm.

### Recommended baseline cadence

| Placement | CTA Type | Goal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top of Page** | Soft navigational or hybrid | Establish authority and direction | Best used for navigation or standards framing |
| **After Intro** | Soft or hybrid | Micro-commitment or guided next step | Can offer guide, checklist, or routing help |
| **Middle of Page** | Hybrid | Convert proof and criteria into movement | Best place for cluster routing or tool engagement |
| **Bottom of Page** | Firm or strongest earned CTA | Capture highest-intent reader | Must feel like the logical next move |

### Top-of-page rule
The top CTA should usually:
- help the reader orient
- deepen engagement
- route by question or need

It should not usually hard-close.

### Mid-page rule
The mid-page CTA should appear after:
- a major proof section
- a standards section
- a mechanism explanation
- or a trust-building block

This is often where the page converts curiosity into direction.

### Bottom-of-page rule
The bottom CTA should be the strongest ask the page has earned.

Not the strongest ask the template prefers.

### High-value page modifier
If the pillar page is strategically important, high-value, or high-difficulty, CTA language may become firmer in the middle and bottom sections.

But:
- this does not justify premature hard-close language
- this does not override reader-state fit
- this does not remove the requirement to earn trust first

## 6.0 TRUST SIGNAL REQUIREMENTS

Because Pillar pages introduce the category standard and the brand's core philosophy, trust must focus on authority, engineering, proof, and why the source deserves belief.

Required trust mix:
1. **Why-we-know block** — history, engineering authority, original mechanism, or experience base
2. **Specific proof** — credible data, testing, source-backed claims, or field observation
3. **Risk-reducing signal** — guarantee, fit logic, expectation-setting, inspection guidance, or quality assurance

### Authority rule
Authority should feel specific and human.

Use a flexible combination of:
- named expert or writer context
- one attributed quote
- one concrete proof element

Do not force the same trust block pattern on every page.

---

## 7.0 KEY TAKEAWAYS ALIGNMENT

Pillar pages may include a Key Takeaways section, but this module does not own Key Takeaways structure.

Key Takeaways is governed upstream by strategy and execution planning.

### Conversion rule for Key Takeaways
If present, Key Takeaways must support the page's conversion path by compressing:
- the broad problem or tension
- the standards or criteria that matter
- why weak options fail
- why this solution or system is stronger
- the best next step

Key Takeaways must not function as a detached summary that removes the need to continue.

---

## 8.0 ZERO-CLICK SURVIVAL INTEGRATION

To resist AI extraction and still create movement, every Pillar page must include at least one page element that creates action value beyond a summary answer.

Approved formats include:
- diagnostic tool
- rainfall or fit calculator
- standards checklist
- evaluation guide
- routing aid to the right cluster
- interactive or semi-interactive next-step tool

### Rule
Do not force gimmicky tools.
The element must feel like a natural extension of the page's actual argument.

---

## 9.0 PRICING AND ROI BOUNDARY

Pillar pages may briefly mention ROI.
They must not become pricing pages.

### Allowed
- brief ROI framing
- long-term value framing
- cost-of-failure mention where strategically relevant
- value contrast that supports the broader standards argument

### Not allowed
- detailed pricing breakdowns
- making cost the controlling narrative
- turning the page into a pricing explainer
- detailed price comparison battles

### Rule
Pricing questions must route to dedicated pricing pages where the narrative can be controlled more tightly.

---

## 10.0 THE NEXT STEP ROUTING

The end of a Pillar page must never be a dead end.

### Primary route
Move the reader to the strongest next logical step:
- the 3 most relevant cluster pages
- a standards or comparison page
- a fit tool
- an inspection or estimate path if earned

### Secondary route
Offer a lower-friction next move:
- local page directory
- adjacent educational asset
- FAQ or glossary support
- pricing page, when the unresolved next question is cost

### Rule
Do not route the reader backward into vague education if the page has already built meaningful preference.

---

## 11.0 EXISTING-OWNER FAILURE EXAMPLES

Existing-owner failures are allowed.

They may be used as:
- warning evidence
- proof that standards matter
- contrast that helps first-time buyers avoid the same outcome
- occasional diagnostic support within a broader page

They must not be used as:
- the default identity of the pillar page
- proof that the page should convert like a switcher page
- justification for hard-close language early in the page

### Clean rule
Use current-owner failures as evidence.
Do not use them as the default assumed identity of the reader.

---

## 12.0 PROHIBITED MOVES

Pillar pages must not:
- default to hard-close above the fold
- act like direct-response sales pages by default
- stop at education when the strategic goal is movement and preference
- hide all meaningful CTA language until the very bottom
- use a CTA gradient that ignores reader state
- close with a generic summary that creates no next step
- turn existing-owner failure evidence into the assumed identity of the page
- let brief ROI discussion turn the page into a pricing page
